# Independent instruction usability review

> Generated evaluation outputs referenced below are retained locally, outside
> this PR. See the [artifact policy](instruction-generation/README.md#local-artifacts).

Reviewer: an independent critic agent, separate from implementation. Target: hobbyist builders. Review date: 2026-10-01. This is a desk review of rendered diagrams, source code, generation diagnostics and selected LEGO booklet pages. No parts were physically assembled, no insertion forces or stability were measured, and no human builder study was performed. Scores describe the sampled instructions, not certified buildability.

## Evidence and method

The reproducible evaluation is recorded in evaluation.json (local artifact); the source investigation is in [research.md](instruction-generation/research.md). All sixteen final contact sheets in renders (local artifact) were visually inspected: nine repo samples and seven official-set representations. Each sheet samples the first three consecutive steps, a quarter point, three consecutive midpoint steps, three consecutive late steps and the final step: eleven views per model, 176 views total. This covers several transitions, but does not establish that every intermediate page works. Original 640 × 480 PNGs and HTML are under `.local/instructions/renders`; selected house, lighthouse, windmill, train, London, shark and Technic views were also inspected at original size.

Generated views use a blank backdrop, black outlines, cumulative geometry, strongly dimmed previous parts and a textual part/count list. They are review renders, not screenshots of the entire instruction editor. Editability, interactive rotation and the ability to disable dimming can help a reviewer repair a plan; this review does not assume those actions have already repaired the published sequence.

The rubric considers six things: identifying the needed parts; seeing their placement and orientation; coherent small batches; continuity of view and construction task; plausible connected construction with clear handling of separate assemblies; and honest handling of uncertainty. The holistic scale is **1: unsuitable without major reconstruction; 2: substantial editing needed; 3: useful editable draft; 4: plausible independent hobbyist guide after limited review; 5: polished guide supported by builder testing**. A 5 cannot be justified by this desk review alone.

None of the nine samples earns a 4: pictorial identification, context clarity and exception handling still require deliberate review. The best evidence is for ordinary architectural growth and large studs-up vehicle body additions. The weakest evidence is for small objects spread across a large scene and for attachment operations that need more than a final pose.

## Common findings and recommended improvements

1. **Add a local detail view while retaining a small context view.** Whole-model framing prevents clipping but makes individual flowers, panes, clips and studs too small. The house's steps 2–3 and 104 demonstrate this directly.
2. **Provide pictorial parts lists with names and colour labels.** `3005.dat / colour 70` is useful to an LDraw author, but a hobbyist must translate it into a brown 1 × 1 brick. LEGO booklet trays show actual piece shapes and quantities.
3. **Try opaque pale previous parts as an alternative to transparency.** Dimming makes additions stand out, but layered transparent studs and walls interfere with depth and attachment reading. Keep dimming user adjustable.
4. **Prefer a functional section before unrelated detail.** A local distance limit does not prevent the early house sequence from switching between foundation and yard objects. Subassembly or region ownership should be a stronger task-level preference.
5. **Represent separate assemblies and show action guidance where fastening differs from a familiar top-down stud placement.** Detached subassembly merging, underside work, clip engagement and hinge rotation need explicit diagrams or notes with part-specific directions. Arrows are not necessary for every ordinary brick placement.
6. **Make the order inside a batch explicit where it matters.** The planner marks an addition as built while it is still selecting the same step; subsequent additions can depend on it. A single cumulative diagram does not tell the reader that internal order. Split such a group or show numbered substeps; validating all apparent reader orders is a separate capability.
7. **Treat connection and visibility diagnostics as review prompts.** Known final contacts and lower-neighbour order do not validate a stable intermediate model, reachable insertion path, or a usable page. Three-point box ray samples can miss mesh-level occlusion and small visible placement features. A repeated generic instruction to hold an unconfirmed section does not explain where to hold it or establish that it can be held safely.
8. **Separate physical inventory from drawing decomposition.** Flexible-tube and spring representations need ownership by one physical element with a route/installation operation. Their graphical segments should not appear as individually collectible loose bricks.

Two issues found during this review were corrected before the final review renders: saved grass backdrops initially obscured instruction views, and aggregate batch visibility could allow a completely hidden individual addition to pass. Publication and evaluation now request a blank backdrop, and the visibility flag considers completely hidden additions individually. These fixes improve the evidence; they do not remove the need for detailed placement review.

The books finish their principal sequences at step 99 for London (p128), 78 for Mountain Hut (p74), and 85 for Deep Sea Creatures (p62). These are not equivalent to generated step totals: LEGO also uses inset operations and separately numbered subbuilds. Counting main labels alone understates official construction work.

The OMR pairwise ordering agreements are approximately 84.5% for 6350, 68.0% for 6450, 72.8% for 8832, 77.5% for 31025 and 32.7% for 31088. Those values coexist with poor or incomplete visual guides: especially 8832 demonstrates that reasonable agreement cannot certify understandable mechanism instructions. London and 6361 have no comparable authored ordering pairs in these source files; no agreement should be inferred.

Agrawala et al.'s assembly-instruction work supports grouping by assembly hierarchy, maintaining visible new parts and avoiding tedious fragmentation. Its finding favouring action diagrams does not imply that every LEGO placement needs an arrow: the paper itself notes that ordinary LEGO fastening is familiar. The difficult cases here are specifically separate assemblies, flips and nonvertical attachment. Ono and Testuz study constructing brick sculptures from voxels; their connectivity/layout techniques do not establish that arbitrary imported articulated LEGO models can be instructed bottom up.

## Assessment of the additional proposed algorithm

The user's [additional theoretical report](instruction-generation/lego_instruction_generation_heuristic.md) is useful as a design roadmap. Its workbench state, separate build/join/reorient actions, distinction between final contacts and operation-specific precedence, and placement-information objective directly address the observed train, shark and Technic failures. Its requirement to validate every apparent addition order, or number the substeps, identifies a real limitation of the current grouped cumulative pictures. These are substantive model/planner changes rather than cosmetic camera choices.

The report's distinction between physical rotation and camera rotation is particularly important: the current note that a view turns can help orient the reader, but cannot substitute for LEGO's explicit assembly flip icon. A camera can reveal a surface while the assembly still needs a safe reorientation and handling plan.

A bounded beam search is a reasonable future experiment after executable actions and trustworthy rejection tests exist. Increasing search breadth alone would not repair unknown connector metadata, graphical segments in inventory, or missing insertion/handling semantics. The proposed widths and group caps are unbenchmarked starting points, not findings from this review. Priority should be clear detail views and part identification, then explicit subassembly/action semantics, then narrow validated motion/access templates and builder feedback. The accompanying [assessment](instruction-generation/additional-report-assessment.md) appropriately keeps the proposed physics-aware system separate from the delivered heuristic.

## Limits

Coverage means every imported occurrence appears once; it does not mean every occurrence is a separate physical LEGO piece. Custom drawing geometry, springs and flexible hoses can expand into many occurrences. The evaluation's three largest repo templates, town (6,083 occurrences), cathedral (11,817) and harbour (6,966), are tested but rejected by the documented 5,000-occurrence generation budget; they have no generated pages to score. Official models that exceed publication budgets can be planned and sampled but cannot be described as fully publishable guides with the current limits.

Deep Sea Creatures' zero verified coverage includes source matrices rounded too coarsely to pass the existing rigid-transform verification. The feature preserves that uncertainty instead of changing source geometry or claiming approximate matrices are verified. That is an appropriate honesty decision; it does not make the resulting unconfirmed sequence useful to build.

The appropriate feature promise is an editable heuristic starting point with review diagnostics. A claim of LEGO-quality, physically validated or builder-tested instructions would exceed the evidence.
