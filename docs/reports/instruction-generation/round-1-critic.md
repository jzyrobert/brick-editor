# Independent critic: refinement round 1

> Generated evaluation outputs referenced below are retained locally, outside
> this PR. See the [artifact policy](README.md#local-artifacts).

Reviewer: the independent critic agent, separate from implementation. Date: 2026-10-01. Audience: hobbyist builders. This is a desk review of the frozen round-1 implementation and actual rendered evidence. It is not a physical build study, insertion-path test, stability test or human usability experiment.

## Evidence and rubric

I inspected all sixteen round-1 contact sheets: nine repository samples and seven official-set representations. Each contains eleven selected cumulative views: the first three, a quarter point, three consecutive midpoint views, three consecutive late views and the final step, giving 176 sampled views. The round-1 evidence (local artifact) and evaluation (local artifact) are saved in the repository. Original images and publication-style HTML are in `.local/instruction-round1/renders`; selected house and train images and locator insets were also examined at original size. Sampling is not an audit of every step.

The [baseline review](../instruction-critic.md) supplies the research and LEGO PDF comparisons. I retain its six concerns: part identification; placement and orientation; coherent small batches; continuity of view and task; plausible connected construction and separate assemblies; and honest uncertainty. Scores mean **1: major reconstruction required; 2: substantial editing; 3: useful editable draft; 4: plausible independent hobbyist guide after limited review; 5: polished guide supported by builder testing**. A desk review cannot justify a 5.

Round 1 changes presentation. It does not change the underlying part order, batch membership or total steps. The render now uses opaque pale previous geometry, local framing with a context inset, and pictorial trays with names, colours and quantities when catalogue metadata exists. Improvements in legibility should therefore be distinguished from improvements in construction semantics.

## What improved in the actual pictures

The strongest result is train steps 121–123 (local artifact). The baseline camera fitted the whole track and made the green carriage additions almost indistinguishable. The new main image shows the green wall and surrounding studs at a useful size. The small whole-track locator identifies the station region. This repairs a real obstacle to reading the instructions, even though vehicle and track construction are still not separate workbench tasks.

The house (local artifact) shows the other presentation gains clearly. Yard additions in steps 2–3 now have countable stud positions; the roof sequence at steps 79–81 remains easy to follow; the sampled trays have actual part shapes, quantities, names and colour labels. Opaque pale walls expose front surfaces without the baseline's stack of transparent back edges. Lighthouse rock work in steps 43–45 and playground details in steps 31–33 and 46–48 also benefit substantially.

Two defects were found during the initial round-1 inspection and corrected before this frozen review. The initial thumbnail lookup omitted the packaged curated catalogue, producing “No picture” for common sample parts. The context rectangle initially had a horizontally mirrored projection. The final house trays show the pictures, and the final house and train locators surround the correct regions. These are verified corrections, not promises for a later round.

Pictorial coverage remains uneven for imported official models and uncommon decorative sample parts. The final jeep sheet still has missing pictures/raw identifiers for some tyres, mudguards and the windscreen; the windmill lacks several animal-part pictures. The official Technic, police and shark trays frequently retain “No picture” and identifiers such as `3707`, `3820` or `26604`. The full main diagram helps an experienced builder, but it does not replace a reliable small-part identification tray.

Local framing cannot reveal a concealed placement by itself. House step 104 still does not clearly identify which window openings receive the two clear panes. Cafe step 124 and train step 160 have similar transparent-piece ambiguity. Shark step 61 lists several additions while the visible image gives little or no placement information for them. The warnings appropriately preserve uncertainty; a builder still needs another view or explicit destination guidance.

Compared with LEGO's PDFs, the new pictorial trays and larger isolated detail areas close an obvious presentation gap. London p36, steps 6–7, shows the same useful principle: small, readable base additions with a consistent view and a pictorial tray. The generator's early London base pictures now follow that principle more closely. Its graphical tube-segment steps remain a different kind of failure.

Mountain Hut p71, step 77, assembles the bird in five inset operations and then joins it to the mountain with an arrow. Round 1 enlarges the final location but still has no equivalent workbench sequence. Deep Sea Creatures p16 shows enlarged isolated subbuilds; p26 includes three inset operations and a physical flip; p36 builds a separate jaw; p63 shows a tail join/rotation. The generated close-ups improve size, but do not supply these actions. A camera turn is still distinct from asking the builder to turn an assembly over.

The booklet's main step counts remain unsuitable as a standalone quality metric: LEGO also uses inset operations and separately numbered builds. OMR ordering agreement likewise does not validate pictorial usability or physical feasibility. Since round 1 does not change the source order, neither comparison should be reported as a new sequencing result.

Galaxy Commander 6980 remains **unscored** because strict diagram rendering refused unresolved body colours. The large town, cathedral and harbour templates remain **unscored** because generation rejects their occurrence counts above the documented budget. London and Mountain Hut default plans still exceed the current publication step limit. No unavailable diagrams receive invented scores.

## Priorities for the next deterministic rounds

1. **Require batch eligibility against the built state at the start of a step.** Car step 2, windmill frame/pane step 29 and lighthouse step 64 illustrate why a picture can contain an apparent order that has not been checked. If one addition depends on another new addition, split them or number actual suboperations. Unknown attachment should remain unknown; a spatial neighbour is not automatically a usable connector.
2. **Keep a local construction task persistent.** Prefer finishing the current section before unrelated decoration. Check castle's tree/wall mixture, windmill's tower/pen change and train's multiple vehicles and scene sections. Shark steps 41–43 are an especially clear body-to-crab switch. Keep representative consecutive runs for comparison after order changes.
3. **Do not interpret an improved batch as a subassembly plan.** Shark and Technic need real owned modules, local build poses and explicit joins. A hierarchy-based conservative workbench sequence is useful progress if each physical leaf is owned exactly once and joins do not add the same inventory again. A join may contain zero newly introduced parts; it must still be representable as a distinct action. Mark merge feasibility UNKNOWN when insertion/access is unverified.
4. **Separate physical inventory from flexible/drawing geometry.** London's tube segments and the Technic yellow segment sequence need ownership at the physical-element level. Do not solve them by silently treating every graphical occurrence as a part or relaxing verified-connection checks.
5. **Add destination cues or alternate views for actual visibility exceptions.** Clear panes, white parts on pale reference geometry and concealed Shark additions need more than larger crops. A visible final endpoint alone is also insufficient to explain a difficult insertion. Complete the uncommon-part pictorial metadata where available.

The user's [additional theoretical report](lego_instruction_generation_heuristic.md) provides relevant action and ownership concepts for priorities 1–4. Its proposed beam width and batch cap remain unbenchmarked parameters. The next review should evaluate changed pictures and sequences rather than infer usability from added planner complexity. Round 1 is a substantial presentation improvement and a better editable heuristic draft; it does not yet justify a physically validated instruction claim.
