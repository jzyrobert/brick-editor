Reviewer: independent critic agent, separate from implementation. Date: 2026-10-01. Audience: hobbyist builders. This is a desk review of `connected-bottom-up-v8`, continuous triangle-surface translation checks, and actual generated diagrams. No physical build, force/clearance measurement, stability test or human usability study was performed.

**Verdict:** the new checker provides useful, bounded, path-specific CAD feedback and retains the earlier targeted ordering repairs. The rendered instructions remain editable drafts, with the same usability scores as round 6. A reproduced extreme-world-coordinate false CLEAR was repaired and independently rechecked: out-of-domain models now return UNKNOWN before any pair-query shortcut. The blocker-label and phone-status presentation repairs were also directly rechecked. None of these findings authorizes calling CAD BLOCKED a physically impossible LEGO operation.

## Checker review and adversarial evidence

The engine expands the pinned LDraw polygon sources, disabling Play's stud/tube omission. It retains openings rather than replacing receiving bodies with convex hulls. Continuous translating-triangle SAT intervals find an obstacle between endpoints without fixed-time sampling. BVH and swept boxes bound candidate work. Missing dependencies, unsupported texture/flexible branches, unsupported moving poses and exhausted work return UNKNOWN. An external start beyond the complete moving group's model-bound extent addresses the otherwise missed case of a contained part crossing a closed wall during extraction.

CLEAR concerns one tested fixed-orientation CAD surface route with explicit permitted-contact conventions. Parallel/coplanar sliding is treated as tangency; up to 0.05 LDU terminal contact and a small verified stud/antistud mating neighbourhood are allowed. This is not a watertight-solid, material-clearance or real fitting certificate. It does not determine roll, receiving-hole compatibility, snap/clutch forces, flexibility, hands or support. Directions are limited verified underside or source-reviewed axial candidates; an untested direction, rotation or articulated operation is not ruled out by BLOCKED. Whole workbench joins are replayed as rigid groups. Other additions in an unordered batch are conservatively checked at their final positions, rather than omitted as though a convenient within-step order had been established.

I independently ran the initial nine focused collision tests and the latest **ten-test** suite after the numeric repair; both passed. They cover a thin midpath obstacle, outside-start closed containment, an open versus filled receiving hole, grazing versus penetration, missing geometry/texture/work budget, real stud seating with an extra receiver wall, a terminal wall just beyond the permitted mating neighbourhood, edit/native invalidation and rigid workbench replay. I did not independently run the entire application suite; the implementation agent separately reported 904 unit/CLI and 19 browser checks passing.

Three code-review findings were repaired during implementation: the persisted fingerprint now includes full-pack provenance, checker policy and limits; geometry preparation now has a global source-character/cache bound; and moving contact exemptions require connector-local triangle vertices at both ends of the actual collision interval, rather than only at the final pose. The extra-wall tests are meaningful guards against exempting the entire receiving body. No blanket axle/bush exemption was added.

The latest reviewed limits include 50,000-LDU source coordinates and one-million-LDU world coordinates, 20 million source characters, 500,000 cached triangles, 50,000 triangles per mesh, 100,000 detailed pair queries, two million broad-phase queries and one million final triangle tests, with bounded planning allocation and an 8,000-LDU maximum travel. Finite work does not imply a responsive editor: measured pure corpus generation ranges roughly 0.4–13.1 seconds, and generation remains synchronous. A worker or yielding/cancellable execution remains a practical follow-up. Budget flags can reflect preparation or planning exhaustion even when final triangle counts are below one million; UNKNOWN is not a measured collision failure.

### Repaired numeric false CLEAR

I registered identical closed, twelve-triangle unit cuboids in an isolated Node process using the same synthetic-source mechanism as the unit tests. Two coincident cuboids at world offset zero correctly produce BLOCKED for +X extraction, distance 3. Translating **both occurrence poses** to `[1e17,1e17,1e17]`, while retaining their source geometry and relative arrangement, produces **CLEAR, distance 1**. The original local-source-coordinate limit did not bound occurrence/world coordinates; world bounds, the external-start margin and relative displacement calculations lost meaningful precision.

This was an actual false clear under the supported code interface. The repair adds a one-million-LDU world-coordinate limit and a complete-model numerical-domain guard before any path/pair check. That guard also covers transformed body bounds and prevents rounded AABBs from bypassing UNKNOWN through the disjoint-box shortcut. I independently reran the same coincident-cube reproduction: at zero it remains BLOCKED, while both poses at `1e17`, only the mover at `1e17`, and only the obstacle at `1e17` all return UNKNOWN with the numerical-domain reason. The latest ten-test suite also passes. The supported bound is now part of the fingerprint. This resolves the reproduced defect; it is not an exhaustive proof against every numerical or geometry error.

## Builder wording and visual findings

A separate real pinned axle/bush fixture reports CAD crossings by the required receiving axle in both axial directions. I independently reproduced those results with the full pinned source geometry. This can reflect the authored CAD/contact/roll situation; it does not establish a diagnosed physical cause or imply that real LEGO bushes cannot be fitted. **Keep the required axle in place.** Review alignment, intended contact fit or another assembly motion. The implemented replacement wording correctly states a CAD surface crossing on the tested route, preserves receiving/support parts and leaves physical fit undetermined. The earlier suggestion to reorder obstructions would have been misleading for this case.

Ordinary plate/brick green arrows provide useful directional information, while B marks can identify a CAD crossing body. They must remain separate from numbered final-destination markers and dashed alignment references. ## LEGO booklet comparison and priorities

The primary LEGO anchors reviewed in earlier rounds remain the appropriate qualitative standard: London p36 combines a compact base operation with a part tray; Hut p71 step 77 builds the bird in five ordered insets then shows placement; Shark p26 step 23 supplies ordered suboperations and a physical flip. The new CAD checks help answer whether one fixed straight route crosses saved geometry. They do not supply those missing operation sequences, physical flips, receiving interfaces or separate placements. No primary LEGO 8832 PDF was acquired, so that model is compared to the OMR sequence and these operation exemplars, not a claimed 8832 page-by-page booklet analysis.

Priorities are: retain the numeric-domain and presentation regression guards; shorten repeated captions while keeping CAD outcomes visible; preserve unknown/support boundaries rather than exempting whole receivers; move bounded work off the UI thread; then address explicit tyre/holder, hinge/toggle, flexible fitting and head/jaw/tail build/place operations. Independent builder testing remains necessary before raising any model to a polished physical-guide score.
