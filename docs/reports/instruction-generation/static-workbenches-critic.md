## Rubric and scope

The established 1–5 rubric asks whether a hobbyist can identify parts, understand their order and receiving interface, orient and handle the work, distinguish build from placement, and follow legible diagrams with useful uncertainty. Warnings and more module records do not earn a higher score by themselves. The current whole-model ratings remain Crane 2/5 and Train 3/5. There is no fresh whole-corpus physical or diagram evaluation in this increment.

I inspected all 88 original v14 Crane operations in ten actual gallery sheets, the 32 initial Train operations 83–114 in four sheets, and all 31 same-source v13 Train baseline operations 83–113 in four sheets. I also compared the twelve original Crane container operations 65–76 from the accepted v13 ZIP diagrams. These are real rendered instruction images, with selected raw receiving details inspected in addition to the sheets. The initial Crane evidence is now retained under `rejected-marker-pilot`; its semantic marker defect is historical evidence, not final acceptance. The initial Train scene step 113 is likewise being superseded by a deferred scene placement. The recovered baseline Train command ended with signal 143 after the complete manifest and images existed: completeness can be audited, but this is not a successful terminal CLI run.

The Crane OMR source contains no STEP or ROTSTEP records. Its source line enumeration is not an authored LEGO construction sequence, and an original primary LEGO 6361 booklet is unavailable here. The Train is a repository sample, not a primary LEGO set. The comparisons therefore assess actual fan/source geometry and the prior generated programme, without calling either an official LEGO manual.

The source geometry supports a narrow display association. The 195075b backside is the 56 × 38 LDU rectangle at local Y = 0, with its printed face toward −Y. Both source stickers span the adjoining 2362a/4215a panels. Their world footprint is Y = [−69, −31], Z = [−268, −212]. The right seam is Z = −260; the left seam is Z = −220. The patch widths are 8 + 48 and 48 + 8 LDU respectively. Four pinned source texts, including the backside primitive, bind that interpretation. Coplanarity, finite coverage and nonoverlap are display geometry checks; they do not prove adhesive attachment or prescribe application clearance. “If already applied, keep it in place” is a useful supplied-decoration caveat.

The generator has been repaired to choose the contributing panel containing the centre while preserving both receiving hosts. The final actual pictures must confirm the 4215a caption and the unchanged true-before masks. A multi-panel landmark must not silently acquire a wrong singular receiver name.

The whole Crane remains 2/5. This suggests the final source arrangement anticipates track that is not yet built. There is no known graph crossing or certified wheel/rail receiving interface, so I did not label this a proven prerequisite violation. It nevertheless makes the scene instruction premature for practical workspace organisation.

The proposed repair keeps the complete coach supported/set aside and defers only its zero-new-part scene join until remaining in-place main construction is complete. This is a sound organisational rule for an isolated source candidate. It must retain the bidirectional boundary gate and actual replay/preflight, preserve every leaf introduction, and explicitly avoid claiming rail alignment, support or rolling fit. Final acceptance requires the completion-to-track transition, all affected track views and the final track-to-scene window, rather than just moving an operation number.

Train remains 3/5 pending that evidence. A useful coach bench addresses one assembly, while other vehicle/track handling and physical receiving procedures remain open.
