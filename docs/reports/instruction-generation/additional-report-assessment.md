## Usefulness to this implementation

The strongest contribution is its workbench/action representation: multiple assemblies, explicit joins, physical rotations, support operations and insertion methods. The official booklet comparison independently demonstrates why those are needed. Mountain Hut's bird and Deep Sea Creatures' jaw and tail are built separately, then attached. A flat list of final-pose additions cannot describe that process.

## Source checks

The additional report's two LEGO sources were opened. LEGO describes human decisions about step boundaries and camera angles, with repeated physical builds and feedback. Its history identifies LDD Pro's exclusive use from 2022 but does not reveal its proprietary planning algorithm. These support an iterative design/review workflow, not an automatic sequencing guarantee. [LEGO's workflow](https://www.lego.com/en-us/service/help/more_about_us/how-we-design-our-building-instructions-kA009000001dbljCAA), [LEGO's history](https://www.lego.com/en-us/history/articles/d-lego-building-instructions-through-time).

BrickLink Studio's documentation explicitly limits automatic step division's best results to simple studs-up models and recommends submodels and reverse unbuilding. That agrees with the limits observed in the official-model corpus. [Studio automatic instructions](https://studiohelp.bricklink.com/hc/en-us/articles/7123910550039-Automatic-instructions-Divide-into-steps).

Liu, Chen, Zhao and Liu's **Physics-Aware Combinatorial Assembly Sequence Planning using Data-free Action Masking** was downloaded and read, including its action-mask formulation and limitations. The downloaded current PDF is arXiv v3, 23 March 2025 (the first submission was in 2024), eight pages, SHA-256 `4ac4bc53624696da1608433bf0abc234ff6523a9aecbc47b73e901c621877b16`. It remains in `.local/research/physics-aware-2024.pdf`; the paper is linked rather than redistributed. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10162).

That paper generates a layout and sequence for a target voxel shape using an inventory of standard bricks on a baseplate. Its mask includes endpoint occupancy, inventory, vertical insertion operability and a simplified force-balance stability model, with additional robot constraints. Its limitations specifically identify hierarchical assembly as future work. It is relevant precedent for rejecting physically invalid actions, but does not validate arbitrary fixed-layout Technic mechanisms, flexible elements or the report's proposed general swept-motion/hand-support system. The report correctly presents those broader checks as extensions.

Agrawala's planning/presentation work and LDraw/LPub3D are already covered by the [initial source investigation](research.md). The report's beam width 32, action shortlist 24 and unordered batch cap four are unbenchmarked engineering proposals, not established LEGO standards; this feature does not adopt them as evidence-based parameters.

## Follow-up priorities

1. Improve explanations before widening search: pictorial part trays, local detail views, opaque pale context, placement arrows and numbered internal operations where order matters. Verify improvement with builders, not just fewer warnings.
2. Extend the instruction schema with workbench assemblies and explicit build/join/reorient actions, preserving unique part ownership and distinguishing inventory from already-built modules. Start with a few verified rigid stud-connection templates.
3. Add connector-compatible motion and access checks with explicit unknown outcomes. Keep conservative boxes as broad-phase filters, not final proofs. Force and handling checks require new measured or sourced metadata.
4. Introduce bounded alternative search and presentation feedback only once actions have meaningful executable semantics. Compare success, time and builder errors against the present greedy baseline.

The report is useful for that roadmap and for preventing overstated feature claims. Implementing its full planner would require new action semantics, part metadata and physical validation infrastructure; importing its pseudocode into the current flat step format would not provide those capabilities.

## Deterministic refinement checkpoint

The [four-round refinement record](refinement-rounds.md) supersedes the baseline implementation column above. Pictorial trays, local/context views, opaque pale prior surfaces, prior-state batch eligibility, explicit flexible drawing ownership and conservative workbench build/join replay are now implemented. Destination marks and alternate views explain final locations; physical flips, executable insertion motions, force/stability validation and verified module merges remain unimplemented. Candidate workbenches are gated source sections with unknown feasibility, not verified rigid connection templates. No learned model was trained.
