The rubric remains part identification, placement/orientation, coherent batches, task/view continuity, connected construction/separate assemblies and honest uncertainty. **1 requires major reconstruction; 2 substantial editing; 3 is a useful editable draft; 4 is a plausible independent hobbyist guide after limited review; 5 requires polished instructions supported by builder testing.** The [round 3 review](round-3-critic.md) and [original review](../instruction-critic.md) retain the primary research and LEGO PDF comparisons.

## What changed, and what is established

The final candidate gate requires one internally verified contact component, known connector coverage and a compact source section. Foundations, generated owners and raw geometry are excluded. The planner contracts candidates into dependency tasks, falls back to cumulative construction when contraction creates a cycle, and schedules a whole candidate after its external source support/host tasks. It then introduces its leaves once and uses a separate zero-new-part join. This changes global task order; it is not simply the original v4 leaf order with captions added.

Final retained candidates are deliberately narrow: house roof, lighthouse lamp, playground seesaw and swing frame, windmill sails, and three small London sections. These total eight candidates and eight zero-new-part joins, verified against the final evaluation rows. Car, cafe, train, whole official vehicles, Technic and Shark remain cumulative. This avoids treating a disconnected source group as one completed detachable object. Verified final contact connectivity still does not establish independent stability, a usable assembly order, a grasp, or a feasible merge trajectory. The diagrams correctly retain UNKNOWN construction/handling/access labels and call out omitted source support relationships in detached views.

The actual workbench displays are useful. House roof builds at 59–89 and joins at 90; the incoming roof inset identifies the intended complete object. Lighthouse lamp builds at 89–92, including the formerly concealed black plate, and joins at 93 onto the existing tower before the top closes. Windmill sails build at 87–105 and join at 106. Playground shows distinct seesaw and swing-frame joins at 16 and 33. Consecutive build captions now say “Continue”; the duplicate raster/HTML operation caption was removed from the review layout. These are real separate display states and inventory-preserving actions, not notes pretending a module was built separately.

Numbered destination marks now have a part legend, and a join has one `J` destination instead of a dense column of per-leaf numbers. House glass 17–18, car clear lights 19 and windscreen 20, windmill grilles 92–93 and the train windows benefit. Some legend entries still fall back to an LDraw identifier while the tray has a fuller name; matching them could be easier. The mark locates an approximate body position, not a validated stud, hole or insertion route. Repeated long uncertainty paragraphs also consume space that could support a more focused exception view.

The eight diagonal camera choices, including lower views, improve real exceptions. Jeep's final wheel at 34 is clearly visible from below. Technic hose 39 and London flexible operation 216 show `A/B` source endpoints and an alternate view. The endpoint legend explicitly leaves identity, length and fastening unknown. This is useful location information; it does not establish a purchasable part, connector breakdown or fitting operation. A camera turn is explicitly distinguished from a physical flip.

Two important failures remain. Cafe stair plates 126–127 are completely hidden behind the closed facade in the main original PNGs. Their alternate views show the underside of the baseplate, also hiding the plates. The numbered mark identifies a region through opaque geometry without making either placement or access executable. The successful early cafe interior step 11 remains visible, but it does not repair the stairs. Mountain Hut roof clip 170 similarly has a mark through an opaque roof, while its alternate view looks through the supporting baseplate. More camera choices and markers do not guarantee a useful view when the part is enclosed.

## Priorities after the four rounds

1. **Resolve enclosure access before improving search breadth.** Add explicit closure/insert precedence or request manual intervention for cafe stairs and Hut clip. A mark through an enclosure should remain a failed placement instruction, even when it is projected correctly.
2. **Develop physical operation models for mechanisms and flexible elements.** Provide verified part identity, axial insertion/fastening information, apparent batch order or smaller ordered suboperations, then explicit reorientation and merge actions. Final contact graphs and complete endpoint pictures are insufficient.
3. **Extend subassembly coverage through better evidence.** The conservative gate is preferable to false detached claims, but missing connector coverage excludes useful vehicle, minifigure and Shark subbuilds. Improve coverage and distinguish detachable assembly from separate independent scene objects; retain unknown stability/merge where evidence is absent.
4. **Reduce builder effort without relaxing prerequisites.** Review excessive singletons and camera switching, improve identifier-only legends, and show compact task-level uncertainty with precise per-operation exceptions. Validate the resulting guide with hobbyists and real parts before claiming independent build usability.

Four rounds deliver materially clearer editable instructions and a conservative real action representation. The difficult official mechanisms and the cafe access failure remain documented limitations of the final heuristic.
