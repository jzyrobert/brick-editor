## Reproduced input gaps

The critic found these input gaps through source inspection and isolated browser checks, then sent them to the implementation owner:

1. Run used a click-only toggle and its keyboard-listener effect depended on Run state, clearing held movement during cleanup. It now uses pointer activation with a native keyboard-click fallback and stable listeners. The final regression holds a real touch joystick while a second touch toggles Run, then releases only the second touch and verifies continued movement. During test development the critic corrected CDP selective-release semantics; that harness mistake was not counted as an additional implementation defect.
2. Losing pointer capture while holding flight Down retained descent: the actor moved another 16 LDU over the next six manual ticks. The regression now establishes real mouse pointer capture, forces capture loss, then checks that vertical input stops.
3. Focusing a contenteditable text surface while holding W did not clear input: the actor continued from Z −16 to −32 over six ticks. Normal input/textarea/select focus was handled; contenteditable focus was omitted.

The new `tests/browser/play-acceptance.spec.ts` owns these regressions plus bounded lifecycle and frozen-capture/bookmark/replacement flows. Root implemented the input fixes. All five new acceptance cases passed against an isolated integrated production build, including held-stick Run, Down capture loss, contenteditable focus, bounded lifecycle stability and frozen capture/bookmark/project replacement. Camera bookmark comparisons allow only 1e-10 numerical tolerance for renderer round-tripping.

## Configuration gaps identified and subsequently addressed

The earlier checkpoint lacked custom validated spawn placement, remappable Play keys and configurable camera values. Those gaps were sent to the implementation owners and are now implemented. The final independent browser review at both touch sizes applied camera settings, rejected a pitch outside the displayed degree bounds, saved a supported spawn without moving the actor, explicitly returned to it, rejected unsupported feet coordinates and verified a remapped forward key through actual movement. Authored document queries remained unchanged. The updated UX score is 8.7/10.

Remaining scope is narrower: arbitrary catalogue geometry and physical-device camera ergonomics are not established by these fixtures; realistic vehicle/world collision, riding and pushing remain explicitly unsupported. Camera-footprint coverage belongs to the engine tests and is distinct from this UI review.

## Final focus and hold/release checks

A subsequent desktop review caught an overbroad native-control exception: after clicking Third person, the focused camera button swallowed W/A/S/D. Root narrowed that exception to Enter/Space. The separate `play-keyboard-focus.spec.ts` now passes: W moves while the camera button retains focus, Enter and Space each toggle Run exactly once without vertical movement, and Escape pauses from the focused control.

The critic also held the actual touch “Hold forward” control for 12 manual ticks in the vehicle session at both 360 × 800 and 1080 × 1800. Vehicle position changed from `[0, 0, 0]` to `[0, 0, -20]`; releasing that touch and advancing another 12 ticks left it at `[0, 0, -20]`. This verifies the tested UI binding and release behaviour, not realistic vehicle/world collision. The final mobile presentation score is 8.7/10, recorded in the UX audit.

## Independent avatar evidence

`tests/unit/play-avatar.test.ts` adds four direct PL-04/05 checks. The actual Three.js figure contains exactly five joint groups (neck, two shoulders, two hips), with rigid mesh children and a fixed torso/pelvis. Applying a pose preserves mesh vertex buffers and fixed transforms, rotates a shoulder about its declared pivot, leaves neck pitch unchanged by camera pitch, matches the player root coordinate conversion and suppresses every visible figure mesh in first person. No elbow or knee groups are present.

Session tests independently verify phase advancement from collision-corrected distance, opposing hip/shoulder motion, backward travel heading, unchanged actor pose across camera switches and bounded jump/fall presentation returning to idle. Against a wall with run held, the tested endpoint drift is below 0.001 LDU and limb offsets settle below 0.01 radians (under 0.25 LDU at a 25-LDU limb tip). Small Rapier contact corrections still advance phase slightly; the test does not pretend these floating-point contacts produce exact-zero internal state. All four tests pass in the pinned unit environment. Camera footprint/settings tests are owned separately by the engine workstream.

## Independent PL-06 obstruction evidence

`tests/unit/play-camera-obstruction.test.ts` adds five passing checks against the committed camera implementation. At aspect ratios 360/800, 1080/1800, 16/9 and 4, the actor traverses a 20-LDU doorway beneath a 76-LDU ceiling. Every one of 80 fixed ticks samples a 5 × 5 grid across the configured near-plane rectangle and rejects samples inside any fixture solid. The actor finishes beyond the doorway; source collision vertices and revision are unchanged. This extends the existing stationary corner/ceiling test with movement and the requested viewport proportions. It remains a sampled fixture check, not an analytic proof for arbitrary meshes.

A close rear wall shortens the follow arm and suppresses all actual `BrickAvatar` meshes through scene visibility. Turning away produces monotonic, gradual arm extension, restores the visible meshes and leaves the actor's horizontal position unchanged. No engine defect was reproduced by these five tests. Together with existing slope, step, thin-wall, ceiling-impact and safe fly-to-walk tests, this improves PL-06 evidence without certifying untested real-device or arbitrary-catalogue cases.

## Independent rig-authoring contract review

The new authoring workflow was checked against §19.3–19.5 and the editor's atomic scope policy. Two concrete pre-existing command gaps were reproduced: removing a rig and replacing it with a subset of members both succeeded when an old member belonged to a locked layer. The engine owner added old-member scope checks. The two independent refusal regressions now pass and verify the entire authored document remains unchanged.

A third independent test uses independently translated and rotated group frames with a world-space hinge pivot at `[3, -4, 5]`. A member initially at `[20, -48, 0]` reaches the analytically expected `[-2, -48, -12]` after a +90° rotation around world Y. Drafting and preview leave source geometry and authored state unchanged; saving adds the rig without changing the exported LDraw rest pose. Reusing the stale draft after that revision change fails. Zero axis, limits excluding authored zero and overlapping group assignments also fail without mutation. All three tests in `rig-authoring-acceptance.test.ts` pass.

This evidence covers the named hinge and scope cases, not every vehicle axis/frame or user-defined assembly. The authoring preview now explicitly states that planar motion initially travels along world −Z, regardless of chassis frame orientation; no visual front is inferred. Independent mobile review subsequently completed hinge and vehicle creation at both touch sizes and accepted the refined interface at 8.7/10, recorded in the UX audit.

## General joint editing: independent semantics and round trips

The independent rig-authoring acceptance file now has **12 passing tests**. Additional checks distinguish implemented behavior from schema support: prismatic travel uses LDU and rebases limits correctly when applied as an undoable rest edit; fixed and spherical constraints reject scalar position control and preserve rest transforms over 60 ticks. Spherical snapshots explicitly warn that orientation remains at rest. Authored velocity-motor metadata does not move the rig over 60 ticks, and the snapshot warns that explicit positions drive this kinematic preview. Incorrect revolute effort units are rejected.

All four joint kinds were loaded and rebuilt using translated, independently rotated group frames and a custom joint ID. The round trip preserves group frames and IDs, optional bounds (including an unbounded prismatic joint), motor fields/units and oblique world axes. Reconstructed anchors and posed transforms agree within 1e-9; exact equality is retained for authored frame and metadata fields. Renaming saves one revision, and undo restores the rig. A valid rig with an extra third group is explicitly refused by the limited form loader rather than silently flattened. These tests establish the named domain contracts. The subsequent independent mobile form review completed loaded-rig update and confirmed removal at both touch sizes, verified sliding LDU labels and fixed/spherical limitations, and retained an 8.7/10 UX score; see the UX audit for screenshots and residual ergonomic limits.
