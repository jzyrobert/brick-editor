Smoothing those surfaces did not change their underlying proportions. The compressed face is plausible because the eyes, brows and muzzle occupied closely spaced height bands. Those were proportion choices; the transform restrictions complicated alternatives but did not compel them.

I would recommend three small changes:

- Consolidate the repeated surface guidance into one statement allowing intentional smooth or stepped treatments while requiring coherent proportions. This could reduce surface polishing at the expense of anatomy, but might also weaken pressure to finish exposed surfaces.
- Include existing warning records and their code locations in each check. This would prevent “no errors” from suggesting more validation than occurred, though it could encourage diagnostic-driven optimisation over design.
- Have text searches report attachment locations and connector types where known, and explicitly say when they are unknown. This would reduce bounding-box attachment guesses, but increase output length and potentially bias designs toward better-documented parts.

These are hypotheses to test, not demonstrated explanations of prompt causation.
