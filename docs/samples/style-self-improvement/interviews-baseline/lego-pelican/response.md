Compiler feedback produced one particularly consequential compromise. Actual axle parts overlapped the complete wheel assemblies, so I replaced them with massing lines. Because explicit parts remove intersecting massing, those lines do not establish a continuous physical axle through the wheels. Zero errors therefore did not prove wheel attachment. Nor did it prove that every offset neck section, wing layer or support had adequate stud connections.

I would recommend three small process edits. First, consolidate repeated surface guidance into one instruction distinguishing intentional brick texture from an unfinished manufactured or anatomical surface; this could reduce conflicting pressure, but also reduce useful emphasis on finishing. Second, clarify that collision acceptance does not certify stud, axle or structural continuity; this could encourage better reasoning, but could discourage valid unusual assemblies. Third, include existing warning records and their locations in the current text-only checks; that would expose actionable uncertainty without adding another drafting stage, though noisy warnings could distract from design or promote unnecessary simplification.
