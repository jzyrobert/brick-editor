I would make three small process edits:

1. Clarify that the core subject’s defining proportions take priority over scenery and detail density. This could improve budget allocation, but could also encourage overly sparse settings.
2. Consolidate the repeated surface instructions into one conditional rule: use shaping and finishes appropriate to the intended form; studs and deliberate stepping remain valid. This could reduce mechanical over-finishing, but might weaken the pressure to complete exposed surfaces.
3. Include existing warning records in check results, while distinguishing collision validity from attachment validity. This would expose useful evidence without another drafting stage, but imperfect warnings could encourage unnecessary supports or overly conservative designs.
