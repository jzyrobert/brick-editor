That interpretation was my aesthetic choice. The request’s “ewok inspired version” did not explicitly require a village or treeport. “Theme first” influenced the palette and signature-part searches, and “blend both sides in every element” encouraged integrating forest materials into the spacecraft. “Choose composition from the request” permitted a standalone subject; it did not require one. The prompt also explicitly values meaningful vegetation, routes and interaction. I chose to express those influences through onboard expedition activity rather than a forest setting, and that choice could weaken recognisability.

I deliberately used stepped brick sculpture for the ears, hood and swept outline. I did not interpret any instruction as requiring curves everywhere, hiding every stud, removing scenery or adding a display. “Deliberate steps and studs may remain” supported that treatment. Tiles finished manufactured surfaces and the leaf mosaics; log bricks retained texture. The coherent Space palette, substantial wing silhouette, differentiated surfaces and compact three-point landing support should remain.

Several concrete constraints affected construction. “Whole numbers only” and upright quarter-turns favoured stepped contours and prevented simply turning dishes vertically into ear scanners. Search results supplied the large yellow canopies, printed controls and rocket-tail pieces. The criticism points to a possible weakness in my interpretation, rather than establishing that optional scenery was mandatory.

I would suggest three small process edits:

- Consolidate the repeated scenery cautions into one statement that context can carry essential theme recognition. This could reduce an unintended preference for standalone objects, but could also encourage unnecessary scenery.
- Replace “blend both sides in every element” with guidance to make both influences legible through overall form or activity, beyond colour and texture. This could sharpen thematic reasoning, but could permit weaker integration in individual elements.
- Include existing floating warnings and their available locations in check results. This could expose attachment problems earlier, but ambiguous warnings could distract from legitimate loose accessories or supported overhangs.

These are hypotheses suggested by this build, not evidence that the edits would improve future generations.
