I chose a large, rounded pelican above a relatively small turquoise bicycle, with a long yellow bill, deep orange pouch, black tail feathers and opposite-height feet suggesting pedalling. I added transparent rear supports because of the narrow wheels and tall rider, following the requirement for a “stable footprint.” The prompt did not require a display stand or surrounding scenery.

I deliberately considered brick sculpture, particularly for the wheels and rounded torso. “A deliberately stepped contour is a valid design choice” explicitly permitted that treatment; it did not require it. I combined stepped anatomy with curved feathers, eyebrows, toes and bill-tip pieces, and tiled selected upper surfaces. I did not interpret the prompt as requiring hidden studs, universal curves, removed scenery or an added display. The distinctive bill-and-pouch silhouette, contrasting flight feathers and feet on pedals should remain. Whether their execution looks coherent requires images.

Several compromises came from construction and counting. Upright quarter-turns ruled out freely angling feather panels and other assemblies; I used stepped diagonal massing for limbs and bicycle tubes. Integer coordinates constrained centring and fine offsets, although I cannot attribute a particular weakness to that restriction. Searches returned motorcycle wheels, but I chose larger brick-built rings to suit the proportions. That was a design choice, not a catalogue limitation.

I would suggest three small process edits:

- Consolidate the repeated finishing guidance into one statement requiring a coherent chosen treatment. This could reduce competing pressures to preserve steps and smooth surfaces, but could also weaken attention to unfinished ledges.
- Include existing warning records, with originating operations when available, in draft checks. This would expose information missing during generation, but noisy warnings could encourage unnecessary changes.
- Let part searches optionally return known attachment information alongside envelopes, explicitly marking unknown information. This could improve joint reasoning, but would lengthen results and might encourage overconfidence in incomplete connection data.

These are hypotheses for improving the text-only process, not conclusions established by this build.
