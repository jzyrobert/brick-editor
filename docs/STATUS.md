Raw primitives keep their 2 million batched vertices / 128 MiB generated-attribute bound, and compilation input stays bounded at 50 MiB. Crossing a render budget draws nothing and retains the document and non-graphics export. Performance at those limits is not promised.
- PNG: 16 megapixels and device texture limits. Tiling is not implemented.
- History: 100 entries / about 8 MiB JSON patch budget. Deduplication: 500 session entries.
- Mapping: six part identities; five audited colours each. Other mapped combinations require an explicit acknowledgement. No current availability or universal physical buildability claim.
- Offline installation is explicit. The versioned app, Play/PDF chunks and pinned starter library are cached together; previous snapshots remain installed. First visit still requires a connection. Complete-library parts are not precached: each is available offline once a model on the device has loaded it.

## Stacking by tapping

In Place mode a tap on an existing part now places relative to that part instead of only intersecting the flat workplane: tapping a top face (including a stud) previews the new part resting on the tapped part's body top, and tapping a side face previews it flush beside the part at the same level, accounting for the new part's footprint and rotation. Taps on empty space still use the workplane; tilted or face-aligned workplanes keep their previous behaviour. Browser tests cover top and side taps, and a phone run stacks three bricks by tapping.

## Section cut

A section cut (spec §20.2) hides everything above a chosen height (in plates) or one side of a vertical front–back or left–right cut (in studs), so floor interiors and dollhouse cutaways are visible. It is set from the Camera views popover or `render.section.set({ height } | { axis, at, flip })`; vertical cuts start by hiding the half facing the camera, and "Show other side" flips it. Parts above the cut cannot be picked, captures include the cut and record the clipping plane in their manifest, and Play suspends it because it shares the renderer. A Measure tool gives the distance between two tapped points (model surfaces, or the workplane where there is none) in studs, plates and straight LDU, drawn as an overlay that is never part of the model. "Explode floors" lifts the model's floors apart for viewing (`render.explode.set({ gap })`): floor levels are found from the lowest points of significant top-level submodels (else layers) using real geometry bounds, and every group travels with the floor it rests on; for the Santorini import this yields ground, upper and tower levels. It is render-only (the source and exports are unchanged), editing and measuring pause until the floors are assembled, and Play suspends it.

## Floor guides, floor focus and room labels

The remaining spec §20.2 aids live under "Floors & rooms" in the Camera views popover (so phones keep the full canvas until it is opened). "Detect floors" proposes floor levels with the same clustering as the exploded view (one ground floor when the model has no separable floor groups), and floors can be renamed, moved in plates, added (at the horizontal section cut when one is active) or removed. Floor guides draw a translucent plane and a name tag at each level. Choosing a floor shows it with every floor above hidden (the roof, for example) and, optionally, the floors below ghosted; parts are classified by their lowest point from real geometry bounds, cached per part across edits. Room labels are placed by typing a name and tapping the model (or the workplane); they are screen-facing tags tied to the floor they stand on, so focusing a lower floor hides them, and they can be renamed, re-assigned to another floor or removed. A camera bookmark saved while a floor is focused can keep that floor view ("hide the roof in this camera profile"); choosing the bookmark restores it, and camera collections render each bookmark with its own floor view (bookmarks without one show every floor) and list it in the manifest.

These are authoring aids only. Floors, labels and bookmark floor views are document metadata edited through undoable commands (`floors.set`, `labels.add`/`labels.update`/`labels.remove`, `camera.bookmark` with `floorFocus`, `camera.bookmark.focus`); they never delete, move or reorder parts or change instruction order. They round-trip through native projects, autosave, IndexedDB projects and checkpoints. They are deliberately omitted from LDraw/MPD export and share links (like camera bookmarks), and clipboard fragments drop them. Focus and overlay visibility are view settings (`render.floors.set({ focus, floorGuides, roomLabels })`); captures honour the focus and draw visible overlays, recording `architecture.floorFocus` and overlay counts in their manifest, and Play suspends them. `architecture.get()`/`detectFloors()` and `brick-cli floors --input file` report floors bottom-up with part and label counts. Open: labels are placed at a tapped point rather than by room outline, and detection needs floor-per-submodel or floor-per-layer structure to find more than a ground floor.

## Named checkpoints and change reports

Project mode keeps up to 20 named checkpoints per project on this device (IndexedDB, checksummed, independent of autosave snapshots). A checkpoint can be compared with the current project at occurrence level (added, removed, moved, recoloured, moved to another layer, replaced), the changed parts selected on the canvas, the structured report downloaded as JSON, the checkpoint downloaded as a native backup, or restored after an inline confirmation that offers a backup first. The same comparison is available to agents through `checkpoints.compare()` and `brick-cli compare`. Occurrence identity is the source path, so parts in submodels that are made unique or regrouped read as removed and added; temporary visual diff overlays beyond selection highlighting remain open.

A model-health panel (Project mode, `health.check()`, `brick-cli health`) reports six checks, each with its certainty: missing definitions and unsupported rendering features (exact); connections (verified stud connections; exact only when every part has verified connector data, see [connectors](CONNECTORS.md)); overlapping part bodies and separate touching groups (approximate, from catalogue boxes for upright parts, allowing stud rows); and instruction-plan omissions or stale step entries (exact). Parts outside the box checks (raw, custom, tilted or unmapped) are counted and reported, and a check that could measure nothing reports "unknown", not "ok". Affected parts can be selected on the canvas.

## Connectors: stud snapping, connectivity and connected selection

Connectors are derived at build time from the pinned LDraw geometry (`npm run library:connectors`, pack `ldraw-derived-connectors-2`, hash-locked in `data.json` and recorded in every project's library lock): studs from the official stud primitives through the subfile tree (stretched primitives measured from their top), anti-studs by testing each stud-lattice cell of the body's lowest plane against its actual triangles, and every stud tested for a seat on the body and room above it. Door hinge pins and door-frame hinge sockets are found geometrically, and every part gets body occupancy boxes (tight boxes per stud cell and 4 LDU slab, studs excluded, BFC-aware). A part gets `snapVerified` only when one whole-part rule ties the derivations to its footprint and name; **164 of 214 catalogue parts are verified** (128 before): full underside 118, matched outline 9, solid base 4, partial underside 21 (arches, inverted and curved slopes), side studs 6 (side-stud and headlight bricks), jumpers 2, hinge leaves 4 (two doors, two window panes). Brackets, hinge bricks/plates, clips, handles, Technic holes, wedge plates, round corners and decor stay unverified with reasons. No LDCad data is used; see [connectors](CONNECTORS.md) for the method, licence, hinge data format and gaps.

Placing a part computes ranked **fits**: stud fits within one stud of the tap's stack/beside/under proposal (turn kept), side-stud fits when the tapped face carries sideways studs (the part turns with its up axis along the stud; Rotate spins it about the stud), and hinge fits when a door or window pane is tapped onto a frame (seated closed in each socket pair; the pair nearest the tap first). Fits whose occupancy boxes would pass through another part are refused (exact for any quarter-turn relation, box-conservative for tilted parts), so a plate now fits under an arch's opening or into a headlight brick's recess. The placement card gains a compact **Next fit** button (→ 1/3) when more than one fit is offered; N or Tab cycle on a desktop; a new tap keeps the shown fit while no other is nearer by more than 6 LDU (hysteresis), so taps a few pixels apart do not flicker. The preview shows each fit's orientation and Place part commits it. Health's Connections and **Select connected** follow stud and hinge connections; **Pick a stud** also works on side studs. Automation adds `connectors.fits`, `connectors.orient`, `snap({ previous })` and hinge data in `connectors.part` (axis and pivot for opening doors in Play). Projects saved before this pass gain the connector lock on load with the previous (absent) value in `metadata.previousLocks`.

Evidence: `tests/unit/connectors.test.ts` pins hand-checked extraction for every new family (both jumpers, six side-stud bricks including the headlight's recessed stud, arches, inverted and curved slopes, doors, frames, occupancy boxes) and re-derives the whole pack; `tests/unit/connector-fits.test.ts` covers orientation maths, side-stud, jumper and hinge fits (turned and off-grid frames, the 2 × 4 × 6 frame, a blocked opening), seats in a non-catalogue frame (30179, official files kept as a pinned fixture), occupancy clash tests, candidate ranking, hysteresis (a swept proposal switches exactly once) and hinge counts in health; `tests/unit/catalogue-integrity.test.ts` covers the lock migration. Browser tests (`tests/browser/connector-fits.spec.ts`) cycle fits by button, N and Tab and check repeated taps keep the fit, turn a plate onto a side stud by tapping and verify the placed transform and connection, seat a door in a frame by tapping, swap its hinge side and place it, and check the phone placement card at 360 × 600; `hud-layout.spec.ts` checks the card with Next fit shown at 360 × 600 and 411 × 685. ## Complete official library (2026-09-28)

Any official LDraw part now resolves, not just the curated 214. Pack `public/libraries/ldraw-full-2026-09-28` is built by `npm run library:full` (`scripts/build-full-library.ts`) from the official `complete.zip`, pinned by SHA-256 (`d2a69586…`, 145 MB, latest update 2026-08) in `scripts/full-library.json`, and validated end to end by `npm run library:validate`.

- **Contents.** 36,805 files, unmodified: 24,735 parts, 9,235 subparts, 2,835 primitives (including `8/` and `48/`), `LDConfig.ldr` (identical to the curated pack's), `CAreadme.txt`, `CAlicense4.txt`, `CAlicense.txt` and `NOTICE.txt`. Every file passes the same licence (CC BY 4.0) and official-provenance gates as the curated pack, and keeps its own author and licence header. Sample models are not packed; the `!TEXMAP` images are in a separate texture pack (see "Textured parts"). The archive itself references four files it does not contain (from `2374b`, `5241` and `6218741g`); these are recorded in the manifest, and those parts have unknown bounds and report an unresolved dependency when rendered. One curated file (`87552.dat`) was fetched individually from the library website and differs from the archive in header metadata only; the curated copy wins, and validation checks that the geometry is identical.
- **Format.** 3,122 content-addressed chunks (`chunks/<sha256>.bin`, the gzip of their files' exact bytes concatenated): 86 MB stored, 501 MB raw, about 28 kB per chunk. `index.json` (2.5 MB, about 620 kB compressed) lists each chunk's hash, size, file names and lengths and, for every top-level part, the chunks of its whole dependency closure and its conservative bounds. `catalog.json` (3.2 MB, about 640 kB compressed) lists number, title, category and keywords for search. Primitives are grouped by how many parts use them, so hot shared primitives sit in a few chunks; each subpart travels with the first part that uses it; parts are packed in natural order so families share chunks. Chunk boundaries are content defined, so a later release changes only neighbouring chunks. The build is deterministic (same archive, same manifest hash).
- **Trust and locks.** New projects record `library.full = { releaseId, manifestSha256 }` alongside the curated lock. The manifest pins index, catalogue and colour file; the index pins every chunk. Projects saved before this pack are re-pinned on load (it only resolves previously missing parts), with the old lock kept in `metadata.previousLocks`; a project pinned to a different complete pack keeps its pin and its extra parts stay unresolved.
- **Loading.** The curated pack still loads eagerly as one bundle. When a model (import, saved project, share link, placement) references anything else, the app loads the index once, then fetches exactly the chunks listed for the referenced parts' closures in one batch (12 in flight), verifies each against the index, and decompresses and registers the files. The import worker loads the same index so import diagnostics are exact. The fixture's nine non-catalogue parts need 20 chunks (0.49 MB). For about 200 distinct non-catalogue architectural parts, simulated on random selections, a model needs about 300 chunk requests and 7–8 MB (plus the index); 50 parts need about 110 requests and 2.7 MB.
- **Offline.** Every verified file (manifest, index, catalogue, chunks) is stored in Cache Storage (`brick-editor-ldraw-full:<release>`), and cached bytes are verified again when read. The offline app snapshot does not precache the pack. After one online load, reloading offline renders the same model. A part never loaded before is reported as unavailable offline.
- **Tampering.** A chunk (or manifest, or index) whose bytes do not match is rejected, never cached, and a corrupt cached entry is discarded and fetched again. A part whose closure touches a refused chunk resolves as missing (diagnostic box, missing-definitions health warning, strict readiness fails) instead of rendering partial geometry.
- **Bounds and queries.** Build-time bounds (the same rules as `sourceBounds`, rounded outwards to 0.001 LDU) are installed into the shared installed-bounds table when the index registers, so stacking, health boxes, explode, floors, inspector sizes and spatial queries cover the new parts without fetching geometry. Subparts or primitives referenced directly by a model have no stored bounds and stay unknown. Non-catalogue parts now have derived connectors and occupancy and, where the LDraw file names one, a derived marketplace mapping (see the 2026-09-29 section below).
- **CLI.** `brick-cli` registers the pack from disk (verified against the lock) before any operation, so validate, health, query, floors and inventory resolve every official part offline; `render`, `play` and `instructions` use the local server over the same files. Portable exports with `--include-official` take files outside the curated pack from verified chunks.
- **Parts picker.** With a search typed, an "All LDraw parts" line offers **Search every official LDraw part**. Choosing it loads the part list once and shows up to 60 ranked matches (moved-to redirects hidden, curated parts excluded because they are listed above). Cards show a rendering from the complete library's thumbnail atlas (see "Complete-library thumbnails and picker"). Choosing a card loads the part's verified geometry and derives its placement footprint, stud-grid phase and height from the stored bounds. Nothing of the pack is fetched until a model or search needs it.
- **Tests.** Unit: resolution from disk, bounds, spatial queries, lock adoption, the one-batch plan and request count, closure completeness, offline reload from a cache, and tamper rejection for chunks, cache entries and manifest. CLI: health and query on `fixtures/ldraw/full-library.ldr` (two doors including the `3861` moved-to redirect, a dome, a round-top cylinder, two fences, an arch, shutter parts), and a strict headless render. Browser: strict import and render, offline reload with the service worker installed, tamper rejection, and the picker scope. Play: auto-door detection covers the fixture's 3861 (via 3861c) and 60616b, and `brick-cli play` hinges a 60616b leaf in the door room, stops the explorer while closed and lets it through with `--open-doors`. `60616.dat` is not an official file (the official doors are `60616a`, which is in the curated pack, and `60616b`); `3861.dat` is the official redirect to `3861c.dat` and resolves through it.
- **Limits.** The renderer's variant budget is now 2,048 on desktop and 768 on phones (was 128); see "Large real-parts models". Deployment: about 4,000 files in `dist`, largest 7.6 MB; `npm run deploy:check` enforces the Cloudflare Pages limits (20,000 files, 25 MiB per file) in CI.

- **How it is rendered.** `npm run library:part-thumbnails` (`scripts/build-part-thumbnails.ts`) uses the app's own renderer in headless Chromium (SwiftShader) with the curated thumbnails' view and quality controls. Instead of one render per part, each import places 256 parts on a 16 × 16 orthographic grid, every part scaled uniformly so its box fills its cell exactly as the curated framing would; one 2560 px render is downscaled to 80 px cells. Stud-dense parts of 10 studs or more are drawn without edge lines, as for curated baseplates. 15,424 parts whose geometry has fixed colours (prints, stickers, assemblies such as wheels with tyres) are recoloured black and rendered again: pixels that do not change are print, and the difference becomes a tint mask. Bodies are white, so the UI tints them to the held colour like the curated thumbnails, while prints and fixed-colour pieces keep their own colours (15,289 cells have a visible print).
- **Pack.** `public/thumbnails/ldraw-full-2026-09-28/`: 365 WebP sheets of 64 cells (8 × 8, 640 × 640 px, about 60 kB each), 361 matching mask sheets, and `index.json` (433 kB; part → sheet and cell, printed cells). Sheets group parts by LDraw category and number, so browsing a category touches few sheets. 727 files, 24.9 MB in all. Sheets are content-addressed (`sheets/<sha256>.webp`), the index pins every sheet by hash and size and records the complete library it was rendered from, and `src/catalog/part-thumbnails-lock.json` pins the index. `npm run library:validate` checks the lock, every hash, size and sheet dimension, that every placeable part has exactly one cell (or is listed as unrendered; none are), and that nothing else is in the directory. Packing is deterministic (re-packing gives the same index hash).

The render phase is resumable (one cache file per batch under `.cache/`), and a batch that fails is split in halves until the failing parts are isolated. Packing takes about 3 minutes. The sheets are committed like the complete pack; CI validates but never renders. Each scene gets a freshly loaded page: a second large import into the same page can fail to resolve official files (a renderer bug, recorded in TODO).

On phones it is the same sheet and chip row; the HUD overlap test covers it at 360 × 600 and 411 × 685.

## Play figure, smooth movement and mouse look (2026-09-29)

User feedback: the mouse had to drag to look, the third-person character did not look like a brick figure, walking diagonally jittered badly (motion sickness), limbs never moved, and "collision unavailable" appeared when trying to walk.

- **Diagonal jitter, root causes and fixes.** (1) Every grounded controller sweep included gravity's small downward step, so it caught the internal edges between coplanar triangles (brick tops, plate grids) and stalled for a whole tick every few steps; walking diagonally crosses those edges constantly (measured stop-start of 1.67 → 0.06 LDU per tick). Grounded walking now asks Rapier only for horizontal travel and relies on snap-to-ground; standing still and airborne motion still sweep with gravity. (2) Each step-up or ground snap was turned into upward velocity, so climbing onto a stud launched a small hop (up to 16 LDU) that repeated all the way across a baseplate; on the ground the vertical speed now restarts from zero. (3) The capsule's round base still dips into the gaps between studs, which lie on the grid diagonals (up to 4 LDU); the camera and the figure now follow the feet through critically damped "ride" springs (the camera may lag a step, the figure at most a stud), while snapshots and collision keep the exact feet. (4) The figure was drawn at the last fixed tick while the camera was interpolated, so it juddered a whole tick (1.7 LDU) against the view; both now use the same interpolation factor. (5) The figure's heading snapped to the corrected travel direction every tick (up to 0.74 rad per tick against a wall); it now turns with a critically damped spring.
- **Stairs.** Risers up to one brick (24 LDU, a third of the figure's height, as in Minecraft) are stepped up by a swept lift-move-settle probe (Rapier's own autostep overshoots at that height); the camera rises smoothly. Taller risers (for example 32 LDU garden stairs) still need a jump (apex about 37 LDU). The profile's `stepHeight` is now 24.
- **Figure.** An original procedural toy figure built from primitives in the 72 LDU collider's proportions: cylindrical head with a top stud and a simple drawn face, tapered torso, bent arms ending in C-shaped hands, hip block and two legs. Five rigid joints turn about declared pivots (neck yaw/pitch, shoulders and hips about X); about a dozen draw calls, no download. Official LDraw minifigure parts were considered: they exist in the complete pack, but a real minifigure is about 100 LDU tall (it would have to be shrunk to the collider), loading them costs the 2.5 MB index plus chunks for builds that never needed the complete pack, and they are unavailable offline until cached.
- **Animation.** Minecraft's humanoid pattern: legs swing opposite at `cos(phase) × 1.4 × amount`, arms opposite their legs at `cos(phase) × amount`; the swing amount blends toward speed each tick (walking about 0.53, running 0.9); the phase advances with distance actually travelled. Subtle idle arm sway; the head follows the view (50° yaw either side, bounded pitch) and the body turns with it beyond that; walking backward faces forward. Airborne: arms up and forward, more when falling; flying: legs trailing with a slow flutter, a gentle bob and no stride unless skimming the ground.
- **Mouse look.** On a computer, clicking the view (or Enter Play / Resume) captures the pointer; raw mouse movement turns the view (0.0025 rad per count × sensitivity, pitch limits unchanged). Esc releases the pointer and pauses, so every button is reachable. A small "Click to look around · Esc to release" hint sits under the crosshair until the pointer is captured. Where the browser refuses pointer lock (iframes, automation), dragging still looks around. Sensitivity (0.1–5×) and invert-Y live with the Play keyboard settings and are saved per device. Touch drag-to-look is unchanged.
- **"Collision unavailable".** Not reproduced on the live site. The plausible causes are missing parts (one unresolved part turns walking off for the whole world, for example a complete-library download that failed or was interrupted) and worlds over the collision budget. Entering Play now retries loading any complete-library parts the build references and re-renders before collecting collision, and every refusal names the reason (the missing part names, or the triangle budget) instead of a generic message; a world that opens in Fly says why straight away.
- **Tests.** Unit: limb-swing maths, heading smoothing and policy, interpolation, a jitter metric on a triangulated plate, along a wall and over studs, stairs of 8/24/32 LDU, look settings and the collision explanations. Browser: third-person walking, diagonal and flight on a baseplate with limbs moving between frames and the figure steady against the camera; desktop pointer-lock look with sensitivity/invert-Y and Esc; drag fallback when pointer lock is refused.
- **Screenshots:** [walking](screenshots/play-figure-walk.png), [diagonal](screenshots/play-figure-diagonal.png).
- **Checked on the Santorini v4 file:** walking out through each opened ground-floor door, both ways, straight through the middle, no longer stalls. Approaching 18 LDU off-centre still meets the door frame and slides past it over about eight ticks (the controller does not steer around corners).

## Frame cost on large real-parts models (2026-09-29)

Batches are classified once per model update and then refilled in place: instruction steps, dimming, layer and floor ghosting, floor focus, explode and section cuts rewrite instance arrays instead of rebuilding, and ghosted, dimmed and transparent (glass) parts are instanced instead of one draw each. On the 20,000-part village a dimmed instruction step draws 1,767 calls instead of 48,468 and changes in tens of milliseconds instead of seconds; the still whole-model view draws 893 calls instead of 2,192. Above a per-profile size (desktop 4 M, phone 500 k scene triangles) frames drawn while the view moves (orbit, zoom, transform drags, Play) leave out edge lines, and phones draw at 1.25× density, restored 180 ms after the view stops; captures never reduce. The Realistic look reuses its shadow map for camera-only Play frames. Compiled part geometry is indexed (2.9× fewer vertices in the village), and picking uses a distance-ordered box broadphase over triangle meshes (about 4× faster). Physical-phone frame rates remain unmeasured. See [rendering](RENDERING.md#frame-cost-on-large-models) and [verification](VERIFICATION.md#frame-cost-on-large-real-parts-models-29-september-2026).

- **Limits.** Opening a few-hundred-part template used to compile its parts on the main thread (about 10 s for the house on the test VM, page unresponsive); see "Fast, non-blocking model loading" below. ## Lighter handles, prepared frames and adaptive culling (2026-09-30)

On the 20,000-part village the page's JS heap after loading fell from 193 MB to 59–77 MB, the longest main-thread task while importing it from 9.1 s to 0.35–1.2 s (long-task total 15.1 s → 1.4–5.1 s) and while recovering it after a reload from 4.7 s to 0.24–0.45 s (12.4 s → 1.6–3.1 s), measured on the loaded software-GPU VM. See [verification](VERIFICATION.md#lighter-handles-prepared-frames-and-adaptive-culling-30-september-2026).

- **Record handles.** Each placed part is a small record (ID, compiled prototype, matrix, visibility, treatments) instead of a cloned object tree; picking, selection boxes, region selection, collision, bounds and path-traced stills read the record and its prototype's drawables. Real trees exist only for parts that move on their own (mechanisms, transform previews) and in the reference renderer.
- **Prepared frames.** A model's batches are classified, filled and its new shader programs warmed in tasks of their own while the view keeps its last image, instead of all in the first frame; batch draw objects are reused across rebuilds.
- **Off the main thread.** Imports are validated in the import worker; autosave (validation, JSON, hashing, checking the stored copy, the write) runs in a save worker under the same cross-tab lock; the UI no longer deep-copies the document on each change; complete-library arrivals re-derive the workspace once per burst.
- **Adaptive culling.** Large buckets are drawn per spatial cell only when at least half of their triangles are out of view (Play and cameras inside a large build): 18–21 % fewer triangles walking into the village, unchanged whole-model views. Phone GPU benefit is unmeasured; LOD is not implemented.

## Fast, non-blocking model loading (2026-09-29)

Opening the house template froze the page for about 16 s on the test VM. It now responds throughout: parts appear about 0.8 s after choosing it, it is complete in about 3 s, and after a reload it reopens in about 0.5 s without compiling anything.

- **Cause.** 95% of the time was one function: the pinned LDrawLoader's normal smoothing was quadratic in a part's size (seconds for the 32 × 32 baseplate). The loader is vendored with that loop made linear; its output is unchanged bit for bit.
- **Compile workers.** Official part geometry compiles in two to four workers (at most two on phones) and comes back as one transferable buffer per part; the main thread wraps it in Three objects with shared colour materials. Custom parts, raw primitives and parts with file-local colours still compile on the main thread, and everything falls back to it if workers cannot run.
- **Geometry cache.** Compiled parts are kept in IndexedDB, keyed by compiler version, both library manifests, render context and part; every entry is SHA-256 verified on read, the cache is bounded (128 MB desktop, 48 MB phone) with least-recently-used eviction, and quota or storage failures only mean compiling again.
- **Short tasks, progressive drawing.** Remaining main-thread loading work ends its task every ~12 ms. A newly opened model is drawn as its parts compile (most-used parts first, redraws spaced by their cost and by the GPU keeping up), the camera frames the first parts, and a thin progress bar with a percentage shows in the status toast (it stays visible while loading on phones). Edits of the open model still swap in at once.
- **Large models.** The workspace derives the occurrence list once per project (not on every render) and shares it with the rig and layer panels; the renderer and autosave no longer deep-copy each new project again; handle cloning is cheaper. The 20,000-part village shows its first parts ~40% sooner with 30–55% less main-thread blocking.
- **Roadster "Unsaved changes".** No revision was created on load: the save indicator said "Unsaved changes" for every newly opened project until autosave finished (hidden before by the house's freeze, visible for the quick-loading roadster). A project that is unchanged since it was opened now reads "No changes" (then "Saving…"/"Saved" as autosave writes it for recovery).
- **API.** `render.compileStats()` reports worker compiles, cache hits and the cache's size; `?compileWorkers=0|N`, `?geometryCache=0` and `?progressive=0` are diagnostic switches.
- **Tests.** Unit: `part-compile.test.ts` (vendored vs upstream loader, record round trip, transferable views, local colours, missing dependencies, malformed records), `geometry-cache.test.ts` (hash verification, LRU eviction and restore, quota and broken storage), `load-scheduling.test.ts` (time slicing, yielding, the worker pool's concurrency, lazy sources, cache use and fallbacks). Browser: `load-performance.spec.ts` (house in workers, progressive, progress bar, split into tasks, cache after reload, corrupt entry recompiled) and the template chooser now checks the save state.
- **Reopening the page with a large saved project.** The saved copy is verified in a worker and handed to the editor without a second validation or copy, occurrence expansion is about 2.5× faster, and the model loads through the same short tasks and progressive drawing. Recovering a 20,000-part project: longest task 1.2–1.9 s → 0.45–0.68 s, a mode switch clicked meanwhile answered in 2.5 s instead of 5–10 s; a 2,600-part build: longest task 1.1 s → 0.18 s. An import started while recovery is still running wins, as before (recovery stands down). Tests: `startup-recovery.spec.ts` (reload with a 20,000-part autosave: mode switch answered before the model finishes, longest task bound, every part drawn); `storage.spec.ts` now delays recovery by holding the restore worker's reply.
- **Complete-library fix.** A part loaded only as a chunk neighbour of another part counted as loaded without its dependencies, so importing it later failed ("Unresolved dependency: 2313b.dat" after a large import); its closure is now completed.
- **Remaining.** On the test VM the longest task while opening a template is the first frame's synchronous shader compilation (0.3–1.3 s in software WebGL). Very large models still block for up to about 1–2 s in document replacement and the first workspace derivation, and in autosave serialization. See [verification](VERIFICATION.md#model-loading-compile-off-the-main-thread-29-september-2026) and [architecture ADR 009](ARCHITECTURE.md#adr-009--off-main-thread-part-compilation-and-the-geometry-cache).

## Snap together: connected building (2026-09-29)

User question: is there a mode where parts can only go where they attach, or on the ground or a baseplate? There was not (Place snapped to verified fits when it found one but otherwise placed anywhere, floating included). Now there is, on by default.

- **Setting.** **Snap together**, a switch next to the part name on the placement card (and a checkbox in Inspector → Move and rotate). On for new and existing users; turning it off is remembered per device (`localStorage`). A tool setting, not document data: opening or editing imported models (Santorini, templates) is unaffected.
- **Rule** (`src/edit/connected-placement.ts`, [Connected building](CONNECTORS.md#connected-building-snap-together)). A part holds when it clashes with nothing and either a verified connector mates (studs/anti-studs above or below, side studs, jumpers, hinge pins in a frame), or its lowest point is on the ground (y = 0; a baseplate there holds, and parts on it hold through its studs), or, only where connector data is missing on either side, its underside rests on another part's top (reported as an unchecked connection). Otherwise it floats and is refused. Two verified parts that only touch do not hold.
- **Place.** Fits still come first (all of them connect); a proposal with no fit is checked. A refused preview is shown with a red tint, the card reads “Nothing to connect to here — move it onto studs or the ground.” (or “Another part is in the way here…”) in place of the tap hint, the toast repeats it and **Place part** is disabled. Rotate, Next fit and typed coordinates are checked the same way.
- **Moves.** Move and rotate handles, exact move/rotation and the Inspector's Rotate 90° and 1 plate up/down check the moved parts as a group against the rest: refused only when the group held before and would float or clash after (a handle drag springs back, “Not moved: it would float…”). Parts that already floated move freely; parts left behind are not checked.
- **Not enforced:** paste, duplicate, arrays, fill, typed Inspector positions, undo/redo, imports, and all automation commands. Automation asks with `connectors.validatePlacement({ part, position, angle? | basis? })` and `connectors.validateMove({ transforms })`; `capabilities().connectors.connectedBuilding` describes the setting ([API](API.md#connectors)).
- **Tests.** Unit `tests/unit/connected-placement.test.ts`: ground and floating, stud holds (on top, hanging under an overhang), half-stud offset refused, baseplate on the ground and bricks on its studs, clash refusal, door in its frame by hinge pins, unverified parts resting on a top (and not beside or in the air), a verified part on an unverified one, group moves (lifted stack refused, slide along the ground kept, moving onto another brick's studs) and already-floating parts moving freely. Browser `tests/browser/snap-together.spec.ts`: on by default; a floating tap refused with the message, disabled Place and `validatePlacement` agreeing; placing on studs, on the ground, on a baseplate and a door into its frame; switched off, free placement and the setting remembered after reload; exact move lifting a stack refused (`validateMove` agreeing), a slide applied, and the lift allowed with the setting off; a handle drag that would float springs back; phone card with the wrapped refusal and every control on screen.
- **Limits.** The ground is y = 0 only (an upper floor's workplane is not ground; parts there hold on the parts below). Unverified families (clips, brick hinges, brackets, Technic) can only rest on tops or stand on the ground. Support and clutch strength are not modelled: one stud holds a whole part.

## Navigate by default; box and lasso selection in 3D (2026-09-29)

- **Navigate first.** The editor opens in Navigate on phones and desktop, and every newly opened build (template, file, project) returns to it; Escape from Place, Paint or Measure goes back to Navigate, and the Place card's Cancel does too. Select stays one key in the tool column/dock (and V). Selection tools' match, all and connected buttons hold Select for you, since transform handles need it. In the editing tools right-drag now orbits (Shift+right-drag pans); Navigate keeps left-orbit, right-pan.
- **What went wrong in 3D** (measured on House with garden, the castle, the café and a generated 20,000-part village; software WebGL, 1440 × 1000):
  - Box/lasso sat three levels deep (Inspector → Selection tools → Gesture), and while it was on, a tap ran a zero-area region that cleared the selection instead of selecting the tapped part.
  - Through tested every triangle: 0.1–0.5 s on the house and café, and on the village a small box failed after 8.2 s with "exceeds five million triangles". It also used "any triangle touches", so a box on the front wall took the baseplate, long bricks and floor plates that merely crossed it (58 parts for a box that centred on 32).
  - Through ignored the section cut: parts wholly cut away were still selected. Ghosted floors and layers were drawn as solid occluders in Visible and selected like visible parts.
  - Visible drew one proxy mesh per drawable (about 20,000 draws on the village, 4.0 s per selection) and set the ID target's viewport through the canvas pixel ratio, so on screens with a density above 1 (every phone) the ID image was scaled against the region.
  - A single pixel counted: a region grazing a part's edge, or a sliver seen through a gap, took it.
  - Nothing showed what would be selected until release; Add/Remove lived only in a drop-down.
- **Visible** (default): only parts you can see. One ID pass renders the render batches with an ID shader (instanced draws use their own instance buffers and `gl_InstanceID`; merged raw batches carry a per-vertex entry index; ~300 draws on the village instead of ~20,000), depth tested, clipped by the section plane, at CSS-pixel size whatever the pixel ratio. Ghosted parts neither occlude nor count. Rule default **touching**: at least 4 seen pixels inside the region (or all of a smaller part's). **Centre inside** and **Fully inside** (every seen pixel inside and none at the view's edge) are the alternatives.
- **Through**: everything inside regardless of depth, from each shown part's box projected to a hull (clipped at the camera plane, so parts crossing it keep an honest outline). Rule default **centre inside**; **touching** (outline overlaps) and **fully inside** are the alternatives. Parts wholly beyond a section cut are skipped.
- **Always excluded**: hidden layers, floors above a focused floor, instruction-hidden parts, cut-away parts; locked and out-of-scope layers are left out by the editing scope as before. **Only the focused floor** (on by default) also leaves out the floors below a focused floor, so one box takes a whole floor's walls.
- **Gestures.** Desktop: in Select, a left drag of more than 6 px draws a box (a still click still selects), L switches box/lasso, Shift adds, Alt (or Ctrl/Cmd) removes, and modifiers can change mid-drag; Escape cancels. Touch: Inspector → Tools → Selection tools → **Box or lasso select** puts the sheet away and shows a chip (Box/Lasso, Visible/Through, New/Add/Remove, Done); one finger draws, a still tap still selects, a second finger cancels the region and pans/zooms. The view is captured once when a region starts; every move re-evaluates it, outlines the parts it would take (orange, red for removal) in one line draw and shows a count pill ("12 parts", "+ 12 parts", "− 12 parts").
- **Performance** (village of 20,000 parts, 300 variants, 10.7 M triangles in view, software WebGL on the shared test VM): the Visible ID pass is 300 draws instead of about 20,000 and costs a fraction of an ordinary frame of the same view: 2.3–4.8 s against 15.8 s on a quiet VM, 12–19 s against 120–220 s under heavy load (the first full draw after loading pays SwiftShader's warm-up either way). Old per-part proxies took 4.0 s per selection on the quiet VM. Each pointer move then re-evaluates the captured image in 34–60 ms for a 30 % box. ## Build scripts for agents and part search (2026-09-30)

A declarative build language so agents (LLMs) can design large builds without listing bricks, modelled on what makes [MineBench](https://github.com/Ammaar-Alam/minebench) builds tractable: compact high-level primitives, repetition, palettes, a bounded grid, and a validate/render/repair loop.

- **Language** (`src/build-script/spec.ts`, `schemas/buildScript.v1.json`, versioned `buildScript: 1`): studs for x/z, plates for y (`"4b"` = 4 bricks), front −Z. Massing ops (`box`, `wall`, `room`, `floor`, `cylinder`, `dome`, `stairs`, `line`, `carve`) write stud × plate cells; part ops (`window`, `door`, `roof` gable/hip/flat, `place`, `column`, `fence`, `baseplate`, `instance`) emit real parts that carve massing; structure (`repeat`, `mirror`, `group`, components); detail pass (`scatter`, `smooth`). Named palettes with deterministic colour mixes; part references by number, alias, `{find}` or phrase. `interior: empty|fill|solid` (and `defaults.interior`) decides whether unseen volumes are hollow shells with a plate roof, filled with large bricks in a cheap colour, or solid. One table drives the TypeScript types, the validator (path-precise messages), the JSON Schema and `build --reference`.
- **Compiler** (`src/build-script/compile.ts`, `pack.ts`): deterministic; massing is packed level by level into the largest bricks where three plates of one material stack, plates elsewhere and tiles on smooth exposed tops, scoring each piece so its ends avoid joints below (running bond) and bridge several pieces (connectivity), using only sizes known in the colour. Openings of 2×6/2×9/4×9 get windows and 4×18 doors; gable/hip roofs step 45° slopes in with staggered courses, gable ends, ridges, hip corners and chimney holes; mirrored parts swap left/right variants and turn corner slopes correctly. Output: an MPD with one submodel per section (and per component), each section on its own layer. Checks: `checkBuild` (overlaps, grid, verified connectivity; now with a sweep broadphase and connected-group members) mapped back to source ops, plus colour availability, part clashes and opening warnings.
- **Entry points:** `brick-cli build` (LDraw/native output, report, `--render` review views) and `brick-cli parts search`; automation `buildScript.validate/compile/apply` and `parts.search`; **Project → Open file** accepts `.json` build scripts.
- **Part search** (`src/build-script/part-search.ts`): curated + complete library, roles and slang ("cheese slope", "headlight brick", "SNOT", "window 1x2x3 with glass", "lamp post"), synonyms, size/colour/availability/category/connector filters, curated then common (colours produced) ranking with printed/sticker/Duplo/obsolete penalties, loose matches when nothing matches every word, BrickLink numbers and thumbnails.
- **Agent guide and prompt:** [AGENT-BUILDING.md](AGENT-BUILDING.md) (coordinates, ops, interior fill, part cheat sheet, workflow, report, limits, examples, headless use) and [prompts/build-agent.md](../prompts/build-agent.md) (ready-to-paste system prompt with a compiled example).
- **Examples** (`fixtures/build-scripts/`): the House (275 parts from 5.0 KB) and Small castle (255 parts from 4.5 KB) samples ported from their TypeScript generators (11 KB each), and a Santorini terrace village written from a one-paragraph brief: **4,089 parts from a 5.4 KB script** (110 KB LDraw), four hollow terraces, 30 houses from three components, a domed chapel with bell tower, stairs, harbour, boats and flowers; compile under 1.5 s, checks 3–6 s, no problems, one connected structure. The first draft (6,213 parts) reported 5 overlaps (flowers with tabs next to the chapel), 3,745 floating parts (terraces one plate above the one below, a chimney above a roof) and two colour warnings, each naming its op; one revision fixed them and switching the terraces to hollow interiors removed ~2,100 parts with no visible change.
- **Tests:** unit `tests/unit/build-script.test.ts` (schema and JSON Schema agreement, running bond, greedy packing, colour-limited pieces, openings, gable/hip slopes, repeat/mirror/turn, hollow vs filled interiors, colour warnings, overlap/floating reports with op paths, components, determinism and `find` resolution, layers and limits, example scripts and the prompt's example), `tests/unit/part-search.test.ts` (slang and role ranking, curated-first ranking, loose matches, size/colour/connector filters, result shape, determinism), integration `tests/integration/build-cli.test.ts` (LDraw and native output, health on the output, invalid scripts, exit code, reference/schema, part search, rendered views), browser `tests/browser/build-script.spec.ts` (API validate/compile/apply/search, Open file).

## Running trains (2026-09-30)

The question was whether LDraw supports train parts and whether running trains are possible. ## Photo speed (2026-09-30)

User feedback from a real phone: "Photo look rendering currently takes about 30 seconds to render just the buggy." Profiling on the VM found that phones never converged at all: three-gpu-pathtracer 0.0.23 sized its random-tuple texture from the bounce counts, so with the phone's 4 + 4 bounces the tuple that picks each surface's BSDF lobe was read outside the texture and every pixel took the same lobe in every sample (a fixed speckle at any sample count). Its sampler was also biased for the first 64 samples after a reset, every pixel rotated all sample dimensions by one blue-noise value, and a first still linked four variants of the 115 KB tracing shader. Fixed locally (no dependency change; the WebGPU tracer of 0.0.25/0.0.26 needs three ≥ r185 and was evaluated but not adopted): a correctly sized tuple texture, an R2 low-discrepancy sampler, per-dimension blue-noise rotation, one shader compile for the target actually drawn, overlapped with a worker BVH build. New: an SVGF-style denoiser guided by normal/depth/albedo buffers rasterised from the traced geometry, a reference-free noise estimate that stops a still once it is clean (1.5 display levels desktop, 2 phones, at least 8 samples; `pathSamples` is the cap), a traced-pixel budget (1.6 MP desktop, 0.48 MP phones) and 4/3 bounces. On the VM at equal resolution, time from the first sample to a clean still fell from 108–162 s to 17–20 s on the desktop profile (5.5–9.5×); the phone profile is clean in about 20 s where before it never was (cap of 64 samples at 109–127 s). Estimated on a real phone: roughly 3–5 s for the jeep. See [Photo speed](RENDERING.md#photo-speed-time-to-a-clean-still), with before/after images at equal refine times.

- **Tests.** Unit `tests/unit/photo-sampling.test.ts` (R2 sampler stratifies every dimension within a block and repeats after reset; the shader patch; the tuple texture covers every tuple the real shader reads; denoiser edge-stopping weights, backing off as noise falls), new cases in `tests/unit/photo.test.ts` (reference-free noise estimate on a synthetic still within 15 %, stop rule, samples-to-clean, pixel budget, frame scheduling). Browser `look.spec.ts`: a jeep photo still stops on its noise estimate before the cap, within a generous software budget.
- **Unverified.** Times on a real phone GPU and its shader compile time.
