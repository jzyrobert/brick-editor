# Implementation status

This report distinguishes working implementation from the full specification. The delivered scope is a usable core editor with selected later editing/reliability features. **It is not a supported full P0 release, with basic M4 exploration now implemented independently. Selected M4 publishing, the M5 kinematic-mechanism scope and a first M6 dynamic-physics slice are also implemented. The full roadmap remains incomplete.**

## Remaining release gates

M0/M1 demonstrate the architecture and 200-part workflow, but the complete risk-reduction checklist still has gaps. Wider compatibility is not inferred from the starter palette.

- **M0 contracts/conformance:** whole-library BFC coverage beyond independently rendered ancestor/internal-dependency fixtures and broader conditional-line image comparisons, complete custom material/finish scope, source-preserving embedded image assets beyond opaque records, full geometry compiler budgets and reference-hardware measurements. Current tests use a Linux ARM64 VM and software WebGL, as requested, not physical devices.
- **M2 editing:** connected-assembly selection and connector workplanes beyond verified stud and hinge connections (see [connectors](CONNECTORS.md)); general pivot workflows; collapsible folder trees; noncontiguous structural regrouping; globally optimized or connector-aware packing beyond the implemented deterministic allowed-part tiling; favourites/recent catalogue features.
- **M3 reliability:** automatic portable-library installation and audited colour-configuration redistribution; broader stateful-metadata export coverage; comprehensive asset/texture/native migrations; broader storage recovery coverage; user lighting/clipping controls and broader graphics-driver recovery coverage; incremental renderer change sets and broader instancing performance measurements; larger security corpus and physical-device budgets.
- **M3 inventory:** verified composites/decomposition and ambiguity candidate UI; historical invalid-combination data beyond curated coverage; inventory override undo under future structural regrouping; all optional Wanted List price/quantity fields; fixture packs for printed/left/right variants; human-authorized BrickLink destination upload test (**not performed**). No purchases, uploads, credentials or accounts were used.
- **M4:** connector families beyond studs, side studs, jumpers and door hinges (clips and bars, brick hinges and hinge plates, Technic pins and axles, side anti-studs and brackets; LDCad shadow data only with its own licence review); broader texture rendering and offline packs; instruction callouts/arrows/exploded offsets, assembly drafts; remaining exploration acceptance, advanced rig authoring and larger collision fixtures.
- **M5:** implemented items are listed under "Motors, dynamic physics and automatic doors" below. Remaining: PL-03 evidence on real phone hardware; riding moving platforms; seated driving of dynamic vehicles.
- **M6:** a first optional dynamic slice is implemented (rigid assemblies, motorised joints, suspension vehicles, pushing). Remaining: clutch/breaking behaviour, compound-rig and arbitrary-frame authoring UI, auto-rig proposals beyond official door leaves, advanced assembly planning, path tracing, inventory-constrained generation and separately scoped collaboration.

The user explicitly reprioritized working Play ahead of the unfinished P0 gates. Basic exploration now includes Rapier capsule walking, gravity/jump/steps/slopes, safe fly transitions, first-person and sphere-swept third-person cameras, an original rigid-joint toy figure with Minecraft-style limb animation, desktop pointer-lock mouse look, fixed 60 Hz ticks with render interpolation and editor-state isolation. Hinge/vehicle previews and explicit undoable pose application now have browser evidence; broader PL compatibility remains unverified; no complete P0 release is claimed.

## Deliberate limits

- Domain: 25 MiB source/archive input, 100 MiB decompressed data, 10,000 definitions, depth 64, 100,000 expanded leaves, 10,000 additions per command. Some ancillary schema dictionaries have their own smaller caps.
- Renderer (profile-driven, see [resource limits](RESOURCE-LIMITS.md#renderer-budgets)): desktop 100,000 / phone 25,000 physical part occurrences and raw source occurrences; 2,048 / 768 part/colour/context variants; 2,000,000 / 600,000 unique geometry triangles; 60,000,000 / 16,000,000 scene triangles. Raw primitives keep their 2 million batched vertices / 128 MiB generated-attribute bound, and compilation input stays bounded at 50 MiB. Crossing a render budget draws nothing and retains the document and non-graphics export. Performance at those limits is not promised.
- PNG: 16 megapixels and device texture limits. Tiling is not implemented.
- History: 100 entries / about 8 MiB JSON patch budget. Deduplication: 500 session entries.
- Mapping: six part identities; five audited colours each. Other mapped combinations require an explicit acknowledgement. No current availability or universal physical buildability claim.
- Offline installation is explicit. The versioned app, Play/PDF chunks and pinned starter library are cached together; previous snapshots remain installed. First visit still requires a connection. Complete-library parts are not precached: each is available offline once a model on the device has loaded it.

See [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) for the reasoning behind deviations and [reports](reports) for actual verification/performance evidence.

## Editing expansion

Transform handles preview world-space movement/rotation without document changes and commit one undoable transaction. They preserve full affine placements and have numeric alternatives; up to 1,000 selected occurrences are supported. Box and lasso selection was reworked for 3D (see "Box and lasso selection in 3D" below): Visible uses one depth-tested ID pass through the render batches, Through uses each part's projected box, and both show what they will select while dragging.

Nested folders organize layers while global layer order, visibility and locks remain explicit per-layer settings. Duplicating a layer preserves source-aware placements, applicable overrides/groups/instruction plans and folder membership under new IDs; opaque metadata and motion rigs that cannot yet be remapped are refused explicitly. Ghosting is an editing-only view and never alters authored state or exported image opacity.

Scoped LDraw export now retains the selected paths through parent definitions, material/BFC records and embedded dependencies. Deleting a reference removes its orphaned INVERTNEXT directive; reflected raw polygon export reverses winding. These source/semantic regressions do not establish universal rendered ancestor-BFC equivalence.

## Stacking by tapping

In Place mode a tap on an existing part now places relative to that part instead of only intersecting the flat workplane: tapping a top face (including a stud) previews the new part resting on the tapped part's body top, and tapping a side face previews it flush beside the part at the same level, accounting for the new part's footprint and rotation. Taps on empty space still use the workplane; tilted or face-aligned workplanes keep their previous behaviour. Browser tests cover top and side taps, and a phone run stacks three bricks by tapping.

## Section cut

A section cut (spec §20.2) hides everything above a chosen height (in plates) or one side of a vertical front–back or left–right cut (in studs), so floor interiors and dollhouse cutaways are visible. It is set from the Camera views popover or `render.section.set({ height } | { axis, at, flip })`; vertical cuts start by hiding the half facing the camera, and "Show other side" flips it. Parts above the cut cannot be picked, captures include the cut and record the clipping plane in their manifest, and Play suspends it because it shares the renderer. A Measure tool gives the distance between two tapped points (model surfaces, or the workplane where there is none) in studs, plates and straight LDU, drawn as an overlay that is never part of the model. "Explode floors" lifts the model's floors apart for viewing (`render.explode.set({ gap })`): floor levels are found from the lowest points of significant top-level submodels (else layers) using real geometry bounds, and every group travels with the floor it rests on; for the Santorini import this yields ground, upper and tower levels. It is render-only (the source and exports are unchanged), editing and measuring pause until the floors are assembled, and Play suspends it.

## Floor guides, floor focus and room labels

The remaining spec §20.2 aids live under "Floors & rooms" in the Camera views popover (so phones keep the full canvas until it is opened). "Detect floors" proposes floor levels with the same clustering as the exploded view (one ground floor when the model has no separable floor groups), and floors can be renamed, moved in plates, added (at the horizontal section cut when one is active) or removed. Floor guides draw a translucent plane and a name tag at each level. Choosing a floor shows it with every floor above hidden (the roof, for example) and, optionally, the floors below ghosted; parts are classified by their lowest point from real geometry bounds, cached per part across edits. Room labels are placed by typing a name and tapping the model (or the workplane); they are screen-facing tags tied to the floor they stand on, so focusing a lower floor hides them, and they can be renamed, re-assigned to another floor or removed. A camera bookmark saved while a floor is focused can keep that floor view ("hide the roof in this camera profile"); choosing the bookmark restores it, and camera collections render each bookmark with its own floor view (bookmarks without one show every floor) and list it in the manifest.

These are authoring aids only. Floors, labels and bookmark floor views are document metadata edited through undoable commands (`floors.set`, `labels.add`/`labels.update`/`labels.remove`, `camera.bookmark` with `floorFocus`, `camera.bookmark.focus`); they never delete, move or reorder parts or change instruction order. They round-trip through native projects, autosave, IndexedDB projects and checkpoints. They are deliberately omitted from LDraw/MPD export and share links (like camera bookmarks), and clipboard fragments drop them. Focus and overlay visibility are view settings (`render.floors.set({ focus, floorGuides, roomLabels })`); captures honour the focus and draw visible overlays, recording `architecture.floorFocus` and overlay counts in their manifest, and Play suspends them. `architecture.get()`/`detectFloors()` and `brick-cli floors --input file` report floors bottom-up with part and label counts. Open: labels are placed at a tapped point rather than by room outline, and detection needs floor-per-submodel or floor-per-layer structure to find more than a ground floor.

## Named checkpoints and change reports

Project mode keeps up to 20 named checkpoints per project on this device (IndexedDB, checksummed, independent of autosave snapshots). A checkpoint can be compared with the current project at occurrence level (added, removed, moved, recoloured, moved to another layer, replaced), the changed parts selected on the canvas, the structured report downloaded as JSON, the checkpoint downloaded as a native backup, or restored after an inline confirmation that offers a backup first. The same comparison is available to agents through `checkpoints.compare()` and `brick-cli compare`. Occurrence identity is the source path, so parts in submodels that are made unique or regrouped read as removed and added; temporary visual diff overlays beyond selection highlighting remain open.

A model-health panel (Project mode, `health.check()`, `brick-cli health`) reports six checks, each with its certainty: missing definitions and unsupported rendering features (exact); connections (verified stud connections; exact only when every part has verified connector data, see [connectors](CONNECTORS.md)); overlapping part bodies and separate touching groups (approximate, from catalogue boxes for upright parts, allowing stud rows); and instruction-plan omissions or stale step entries (exact). Parts outside the box checks (raw, custom, tilted or unmapped) are counted and reported, and a check that could measure nothing reports "unknown", not "ok". Affected parts can be selected on the canvas.

Camera collections: bookmarks named with a shared start (for example `exterior/front`, `exterior/rear`) form a collection. Photo mode, `render.collection({ prefix })` and `brick-cli render-collection --collection exterior/` render every view in a collection against one fixed revision and return the PNGs with one shared manifest (revision, document hash, renderer, per-view camera); the user's camera is restored afterwards. A collection renders at most 24 views.

## Render looks

Camera views → Look switches between **Standard**, the unchanged default, **Realistic** and **Photo**. Realistic adds a generated room-environment IBL, finish-aware plastic, chrome, pearl, metal, rubber, glitter and speckle materials, and ambient occlusion computed on the beauty pass's depth. It also adds model-fitted soft shadows on a shadow-catcher ground, PBR Neutral tone mapping, and no outlines. Photo is a path-traced studio photograph of a still view (see below); moving frames are realistic raster frames. The choice is saved per viewer. Phones keep IBL, materials and shadows but drop AO and the vignette. Play never accumulates. The looks are exposed as `render.look.get/set`, `render.image({ look })` and `brick-cli --look`. Techniques tried, their cost on software WebGL, and what was rejected are recorded in [RENDERING.md](RENDERING.md). Unverified: frame cost on real phone GPUs.

## Creative-mode HUD shell

The editor shell was redesigned as a heads-up display over a full-bleed canvas (see DESIGN.md and PRODUCT.md). Phones give the model the whole screen: a one-row top HUD (mode chip that opens the five-mode switcher, save chip, export), a right-edge tool column, and a bottom hotbar (current colour, held parts drawn in that colour, Parts/Layers/Inspector). Panels are chalk sheets at half height with a handle; mode cards become bottom sheets; the status line is a transient, always-announced toast; dragging the model dims the HUD and makes it click-through. Tablets keep a 680px floating sheet beside the tool rail; desktop docks the parts inventory left and properties right with a bottom tool dock. Active Play hides the build HUD. Icons are an authored 2px-stroke SVG set. An independent Impeccable finish review scored the fix batch resolved except the tablet docked layout, which was kept as a floating sheet so the model stays visible in portrait. Remaining polish: legacy slate tones and 11px help text inside panel contents, and a text "+ Add" control.

## Inspector and device limits checkpoint

The Inspector now reports batch properties as one shared value or **Mixed**, never an arbitrary member's value (spec §8.4): part, source (official library, project definition, raw geometry or missing), colour, layer, parent submodel, orientation (upright rotations are named; mirrored and sheared placements are flagged), world size in studs/plates/LDU (conservative source bounds including studs; unknown outside the pinned library), and validation notes (missing definitions, locked or hidden members, occurrence diagnostics). A single selection exposes its LDraw placement matrix read-only under an advanced disclosure. Typing a position value sets that axis on every selected part in one undoable transaction.

**Replace part** (Inspector) swaps selected catalogue parts for another catalogue part, keeping colour and layer. Because LDraw brick/plate origins sit on the top face, the default keeps the _bottom_ face in place (`parts.replace` accepts a part-local `anchorOffset`), with a keep-top option. The preview states the count and layer scope, the per-part height/footprint change and a possible-overlap warning for larger or taller parts; "all matching parts" resolves visible, unlocked, in-scope IDs before execution. The whole replacement is one undo step. It does not run a collision check, and raw geometry and project definitions are skipped.

The parts catalogue now supports tokenised search across names, numbers, categories and sizes (`2x4`, `2 × 4` and `4x2` are equivalent; partial sizes narrow), category filters, per-device favourites, a recently-used row and related sizes (same footprint first, e.g. Brick 2 × 4 ↔ Plate 2 × 4). The shown-part count is live. Connector-support filtering is not offered yet (164 of 214 parts have verified connectors, see [connectors](CONNECTORS.md)); a decoration filter is still open; colours are filtered to the held part's real colours (see "Real colour availability per part").

**Catalogue expansion (2026-09-28).** The placeable catalogue grew from six parts to 214 official parts (library pack `catalogue-2026-09-28`): Bricks 28, Plates 35, Tiles 11, Slopes 39 (45°/33°/30°/18°/65°/75°, inverted, curved), Round 15, Arches 10, Windows & doors 13 (frames, glass, panes, doors), Walls & fences 9, Modified 19 (jumpers, clips, handles, side studs), Brackets & hinges 11, Baseplates 4 and Plants & decor 20; some parts are also tagged into a second category (a round plate is listed under Plates too). Search ranks exact part numbers (including differing BrickLink numbers such as 4073 for 6141) first, then name words, then keywords such as "roof", "cheese" or "glass". The old schematic SVG thumbnails drew the same 2 × 4-shaped slab for every part (plates as tall as bricks, at most 4 × 2 studs, no slopes or shapes); cards now show a static WebP rendering of the pinned geometry, tinted to the held colour, loaded lazily. The picker keeps search and a horizontally scrolling category row sticky above a grouped, lazily painted grid (3 columns in the desktop dock, 4 on phones); the parts sheet rises slightly higher on short phones. Placement derives height (the lowest point), footprint, a stud-grid phase and a studded flag from real bounds: odd-width parts and off-centre origins (slopes) snap onto the same stud grid in every quarter turn, side stacking uses the new part's real box, and tapping a tile top no longer allows for studs. Allowed-part fills are limited to plain rectangular bricks and plates. 209 parts have individually reviewed BrickLink item numbers with per-part verified palette colours taken from BrickLink's known-colour lists; five are explicitly unmapped with a reason. The LDraw Trans-Brown (40) mapping was corrected to BrickLink 13 and Clear (47 → 12) was added to the palette. Connector snapping remains unverified for every part.

Device resource profiles (phone/desktop, spec §21.2) are selected automatically and can be changed per device; raising limits on a phone requires acknowledging the impact. See [RESOURCE-LIMITS.md](RESOURCE-LIMITS.md).

## Workplane, model and reliability checkpoint

World, picked-triangle and numerical workplanes control rigid placement orientation, elevation, grid and free placement. Fill previews respect the plane basis and use conservative transformed obstacle bounds. Connector workplanes remain unavailable without verified connector data.

Make-submodel preserves full affine placement and occurrence-scoped metadata for contiguous sibling selections. Make-unique isolates an instance; explicit shared-edit previews support recolour and local translation with complete affected-scope checks. Unsupported source state, noncontiguous regrouping and rig-rest translation are refused. Local-pivot shared rotations were added in the subsequent checkpoint below; general structural editing remains unfinished. Keyboard and UI clipboard now share one fragment; shortcuts are configurable and persisted locally.

Standard, portable, layer-archive and native export profiles are available through UI, API and CLI. Layer MPDs preserve world coordinates. Optional official geometry archives contain exact hash-verified licensed files, dependency closure and notices while preserving official purchasing identity. Extract the archive and configure its library search path: direct ZIP import and audited redistribution of the pinned colour configuration remain unavailable. Native export includes the whole project. Export work is bounded, cancellable and revision checked.

Projects are stored in IndexedDB (`brick-editor-projects`), with two checksummed snapshots per project, compare-and-swap writes and deletion tombstones. Legacy localStorage projects are read and migrated on the next save without deleting the old copy. This removes the localStorage quota ceiling that previously lost autosaves of multi-megabyte imports. Browsers without Web Locks use an exclusive IndexedDB transaction across asynchronous save checksums. Missing coordination disables automatic writes and offers backup; expired locks cannot publish late writes. Other-tab notifications preserve the draft, with fork or backup-before-reload choices. A stale failed save from a previously open project cannot mark the new project conflicted.

Ancestor and internal-dependency BFC contexts now have an original seven-case fixture compared to an independently loaded, hand-expanded MPD from front and back. This closes the demonstrated context propagation bugs, not universal library conformance. Mobile re-review remains **8.7/10**, with screenshots in UX-AUDIT.

## Exploration, instructions and graphics recovery

Play can include one or several authored kinematic rigs. Its rigid groups use transformed triangle colliders and fixed-tick poses, allowing an opened door to become traversable. Swept movement is conservatively refused before a moving surface touches the walking explorer. This is not vehicle/world dynamics, riding or pushing. Moving geometry is bounded to 128 groups and 200,000 triangles; commands exceeding 1,024 swept segments must use smaller targets. Runtime joint/vehicle controls preserve the authored document.

Instruction plans now support create/rename/delete, step add/remove/reorder/split/merge, occurrence reassignment, notes and saved cameras through undoable commands. Camera presets remain accessible in Instructions. Optional aligned metadata preserves older plans, survives clipboard filtering and native saves, and is honored by JSON/HTML/PDF publication. Publication still requires exact coverage and restores the original camera. Callouts, arrows, exploded offsets, assembly planning remain unfinished.

Graphics loss aborts readiness/capture waits and shader polling with a structured error. Three rebuilds GPU state from retained geometry/materials on restoration; repeated real context-loss tests preserve edited documents and native backups with identical restored captures. The capture path owns bounded shader polling because the pinned renderer's asynchronous compiler cannot cancel its timer. This evidence uses software Chromium and does not certify every driver.

## Explicit Play world and instruction appearance

Play entry now accepts a session world profile excluding chosen layers from both visible geometry and collision. Editor hidden/locked state does not exclude geometry by default; the entry UI separately controls temporary ground. A selected moving rig requires all of its member layers. Captures intersect their requested scope with the active Play profile, and exit restores editor visibility. Reports record the frozen inclusion profile.

Instructions can dim previously introduced parts in preview and PNG/HTML/PDF publication while retaining the original appearance of current additions. The appearance treatment does not alter project materials or hide prior geometry. Shared definition editing now includes rotation around a declared local pivot, preserving existing affine transforms and requiring the same all-instance impact preview, lock/scope checks and undo as other shared edits. Rotations affecting authored rig rest geometry are refused.

## Allowed-part fills and rig creation

Allowed-part fills use deterministic largest-first packing of same-height audited parts with quarter turns, optional cell masks, bounded search, cancellable worker previews and one-command commits. Hidden geometry remains an obstacle; unknown/custom-overridden parts block placement conservatively. This is neither globally optimal packing nor verified connectivity.

The Inspector can create hinge and planar vehicle rigs using explicit member assignments, axes and world pivots, with transient preview, revision guards, native persistence and undo. Rig replacement/removal now checks old memberships as well as new memberships for locks/scope. The following checkpoint adds supported edit/removal workflows. Compound-rig and arbitrary-frame UI, suspension and vehicle/world dynamics remain open.

## General joint editing and structured queries

Representable single-joint and pure planar vehicle rigs now support guarded load/edit/remove workflows. Fixed, revolute, prismatic and spherical definitions preserve explicit frames, custom axes, IDs, optional limits and motor metadata during supported edits. Spherical preview remains rest-only, motors are not actuated, and unsupported compound topologies are refused rather than flattened. Arbitrary frame-authoring UI and dynamic assemblies remain open.

Browser and CLI queries now include submodel/selection scopes, counts, source-derived conservative bounds, unknown-geometry IDs, nested unresolved dependencies, physical-transform diagnostics and bounded overlap candidates without WebGL. Connectivity is explicitly unverified until a verified connector pack exists. Source boxes are checked against the pinned library closure in CI; overlap candidates do not establish physical collisions or buildability.

The reproduced deep-occurrence input gap is fixed: canonical JSON paths now have their own depth/segment/encoded bounds, while ordinary IDs retain their prior limit. Aggregate request preflight rejects oversized, cyclic or deeply nested commands before hashing or mutation. Scope resolution uses a prefix trie and exact editing membership uses a Set, avoiding selection-size × project-size scans. Native import limits remain separate. Broader expanded-graph and compiler memory budgets remain an M0 gap.

## Nearby Play interaction

The nearest joint or vehicle among the active Play rigs exposes a contextual button and remappable E action within 96 LDU of its authored joint anchor or vehicle chassis, measured from the explorer's body center. Revolute and prismatic joints toggle between the allowed position nearest zero and the farthest limit (defaults: 90 degrees or 40 LDU). Motion still uses the swept actor-clearance checks and reports a blocked attempt visibly. This is proximity access, not a line-of-sight picking system or automatic door detection.

Vehicle access switches movement keys and the touch joystick to throttle/steering, with an explicit Release vehicle action. The explorer remains on foot; pause, focus loss and released inputs stop driving. Look controls remain available. The authored build is unchanged. Seated entry/exit, riding and vehicle/world collision response remain open.

## Multiple active Play rigs

Play can now activate all authored mechanisms or an explicit subset in one session. The nearest authored joint/chassis supplies the contextual action, and remote controls can select a specific active rig. Door poses persist while a different vehicle moves; all moving colliders and actor navigation share the fixed-tick world. Explicit per-rig API targeting avoids ambiguity. Combined limits are 32 rigs, 128 groups and 200,000 moving triangles; repeated IDs, overlapping members and excluded member layers are refused before simulation.

The legacy single-rig request/report remains supported. Vehicles are still kinematic and remotely controlled from the explorer's position, without seated entry, riding or vehicle/world response. Multi-rig support does not infer joints or controllers from arbitrary imported geometry.

## Animated contextual doors and sliders

Contextual revolute/prismatic actions now travel over fixed ticks at 90 degrees/second or 40 LDU/second, with visible progress, reversal before the midpoint, and an explicit retry after an obstruction. Each accepted increment updates real colliders; blocking retains the last safe pose. Multiple joints/rigs retain independent targets. Remote numeric positioning remains immediate and cancels only that joint's target.

The target API exposes bounded rates, units and moving/blocked/complete status. Pause retains targets while stopping realtime advancement; explicit automation ticks still advance, while image capture locks out mutations. Targets belong to the current Play session and do not modify authored rest data. Line-of-sight targeting, authored seats, riding and vehicle/world collision response remain unfinished.

## Source-only recovery and protected driving

Projects that exceed derived path budgets can now open into a source-only recovery view with local save, native/full-source backups and reopening another project. Scene-dependent API actions fail with a terminal resource diagnostic; status and source export remain available. STEP/ROTSTEP source is retained when editable instruction derivation is deferred. The corrected mobile recovery flow scored 8.8/10. Profile selection/overrides, bounded output and cancellable derivation remain open; see [resource limits](RESOURCE-LIMITS.md).

Supported chassis/wheel vehicles now stop before included world geometry and other active rigs using conservative source-certified envelopes and complete triangle candidates. Neutral release preserves the stopped pose and reason, and reverse/steering input retries. Unsupported driving profiles preserve exploration and authored data. Earlier vehicle/world-response gaps above now have this bounded kinematic implementation; seats, riding, articulated vehicles and dynamic physics remain open. See [protected driving](PLAY-VEHICLES.md).

## Authored seated driving

An explicit chassis-local driver seat now supports collision-checked entry, rigid seated visual pose, attached body/eye movement, driving and safe ordered exits. Rider and vehicle candidates are checked before accepting movement; blocked exits retain occupancy, and foreign doors stop before the seated body. The original open-bench test fixture (now automation-only; the jeep sample has a real-parts driver seat), separate on-foot remote action, coordinate editor, native roundtrip, undo, capture metadata and guarded API are implemented. The seat flow has a fresh independent 8.7/10 mobile review after camera/control-layout fixes. See PLAY-VEHICLES.md and VERIFICATION.md for bounds and exact test evidence. General platform riding, inferred seats, low-cabin fit and dynamic vehicle physics remain unfinished.

## Raw-geometry architectural imports

Architectural MPDs that author walls and details as raw type 2–5 faces (the supplied Santorini v2 set: up to 27,600 leaves, 142 embedded models) previously failed: every raw face counted as a renderer variant (128 limit), each re-parsed the whole project, and autosave overflowed localStorage. Raw primitives now compile through a shared-palette loader path without whole-project reparsing, render through opaque merged batches (transparent faces keep the ordering-safe fallback) and remain individual source occurrences for picking, editing, undo, capture and export. Custom physical parts compile only their embedded dependency closure.

The loader cancels normals of non-certified faces against their reversed twins, which rendered such walls black; isolated primitives now compile without smoothing and loader output is repaired where a normal is degenerate or faces away from its triangle. Raw faces are shaded flat per face, so curved raw surfaces look faceted compared with smoothed whole-model renderers. Browser evidence: all Santorini variants load and render against the supplied previews (software WebGL: complete ~8 s to ready); the complete file survives import → save → reload → edit → save → reload with identical source. Imported doors and vehicles are not given inferred rigs.

Play on these imports is no longer bound by per-frame JavaScript. Before this change, every realtime frame deep-copied the whole project twice (about 0.5 s per frame for the complete file). Rendering also re-signed and re-walked about 20k handles each frame, and any moving rig re-merged all batches. Play now uses a cheap revision read and demand-driven redraws, and caps its drawing buffer at 1.5× DPR. Static collision is compacted losslessly into one trimesh. See VERIFICATION.md for numbers. Walking into dense decorative detail still costs tens of ms per tick on the VM; realtime catch-up is time-budgeted so the world slows instead of the frame rate collapsing. Physical-phone frame rates are unmeasured.

## Connectors: stud snapping, connectivity and connected selection

Connectors are derived at build time from the pinned LDraw geometry (`npm run library:connectors`, pack `ldraw-derived-connectors-2`, hash-locked in `data.json` and recorded in every project's library lock): studs from the official stud primitives through the subfile tree (stretched primitives measured from their top), anti-studs by testing each stud-lattice cell of the body's lowest plane against its actual triangles, and every stud tested for a seat on the body and room above it. Door hinge pins and door-frame hinge sockets are found geometrically, and every part gets body occupancy boxes (tight boxes per stud cell and 4 LDU slab, studs excluded, BFC-aware). A part gets `snapVerified` only when one whole-part rule ties the derivations to its footprint and name; **164 of 214 catalogue parts are verified** (128 before): full underside 118, matched outline 9, solid base 4, partial underside 21 (arches, inverted and curved slopes), side studs 6 (side-stud and headlight bricks), jumpers 2, hinge leaves 4 (two doors, two window panes). Brackets, hinge bricks/plates, clips, handles, Technic holes, wedge plates, round corners and decor stay unverified with reasons. No LDCad data is used; see [connectors](CONNECTORS.md) for the method, licence, hinge data format and gaps.

Placing a part computes ranked **fits**: stud fits within one stud of the tap's stack/beside/under proposal (turn kept), side-stud fits when the tapped face carries sideways studs (the part turns with its up axis along the stud; Rotate spins it about the stud), and hinge fits when a door or window pane is tapped onto a frame (seated closed in each socket pair; the pair nearest the tap first). Fits whose occupancy boxes would pass through another part are refused (exact for any quarter-turn relation, box-conservative for tilted parts), so a plate now fits under an arch's opening or into a headlight brick's recess. The placement card gains a compact **Next fit** button (→ 1/3) when more than one fit is offered; N or Tab cycle on a desktop; a new tap keeps the shown fit while no other is nearer by more than 6 LDU (hysteresis), so taps a few pixels apart do not flicker. The preview shows each fit's orientation and Place part commits it. Health's Connections and **Select connected** follow stud and hinge connections; **Pick a stud** also works on side studs. Automation adds `connectors.fits`, `connectors.orient`, `snap({ previous })` and hinge data in `connectors.part` (axis and pivot for opening doors in Play). Projects saved before this pass gain the connector lock on load with the previous (absent) value in `metadata.previousLocks`.

Evidence: `tests/unit/connectors.test.ts` pins hand-checked extraction for every new family (both jumpers, six side-stud bricks including the headlight's recessed stud, arches, inverted and curved slopes, doors, frames, occupancy boxes) and re-derives the whole pack; `tests/unit/connector-fits.test.ts` covers orientation maths, side-stud, jumper and hinge fits (turned and off-grid frames, the 2 × 4 × 6 frame, a blocked opening), seats in a non-catalogue frame (30179, official files kept as a pinned fixture), occupancy clash tests, candidate ranking, hysteresis (a swept proposal switches exactly once) and hinge counts in health; `tests/unit/catalogue-integrity.test.ts` covers the lock migration. Browser tests (`tests/browser/connector-fits.spec.ts`) cycle fits by button, N and Tab and check repeated taps keep the fit, turn a plate onto a side stud by tapping and verify the placed transform and connection, seat a door in a frame by tapping, swap its hinge side and place it, and check the phone placement card at 360 × 600; `hud-layout.spec.ts` checks the card with Next fit shown at 360 × 600 and 411 × 685. Remaining: clips/bars, finger hinges, Technic pins/axles, side anti-studs (brackets), solid (not surface) occupancy, and adding more door/frame pairs to the catalogue (a library release).

## Motors, dynamic physics and automatic doors

See [Play physics](PLAY-PHYSICS.md). This slice closes the remaining M5 items and adds the first M6 dynamic slice. Nothing in Play edits the project.

- **Motors (M5 axles):** authored position and velocity motors now run in kinematic Play at declared rates (90 degrees/s or 40 LDU/s; velocity motors at their target). An unlimited revolute joint is a continuously turning axle. Swept actor checks block a motor and it retries automatically. `play.setMotor`, the drawer's Stop/Start motor button, and manual joint commands stop a motor.
- **Automation (M5):** the CLI `play` command takes `--rigs`, `--dynamic-rigs`, `--joint-targets`, `--motors`, `--vehicle`, `--open-doors`, `--no-auto-doors` and `--posed-output`. The API adds `play.setMotor`, `play.interact`, `play.exportPosedModel`, `mechanisms.list` and `mechanisms.exportPosedModel`. All requests are schema-validated.
- **Posed export (M5, spec 19.5):** a static posed MPD of the current Play or mechanism-preview pose. The rest-pose export and the project are unchanged.
- **Automatic doors (M5 door hinges):** a pinned table of 185 official LDraw door leaves with geometry-derived hinge axes and pivots (48 explained exclusions). At entry, Play hinges each included door to the part holding it and decides the free swing direction against the surrounding build; two-way doors open away from the explorer. Doors open with E or a tap, from automation, or with CLI `--open-doors`. The `door-room` test fixture (automation only) contains a real 60596 frame and 60616a door; the house, café, farm and lighthouse samples use real doors too.
- **Dynamic physics (M6, opt-in):** Rapier bodies per authored group with convex member proxies, anchored exact surfaces, impulse joints with authored limits and motor efforts, ray-cast suspension vehicles, and walking pushes loose bodies. It is chosen per session under Play → Mechanism physics. Replay is deterministic in the pinned environment. Optional per-rig physics settings (anchoring, mass, friction, suspension, engine force) have an undoable authoring drawer and round-trip natively.
- **Performance:** an idle fast path skips pose sweeps for kinematic rigs that cannot move. Measured tick and frame costs are in VERIFICATION.md.
- **Limits:** 14 dynamic rigs, 64 bodies, 512 dynamic member proxies. Dynamic vehicles have no seated driving, and riding moving bodies is not simulated. Door leaves outside the curated pack now render and collide through the complete official library (see below); a CLI test opens 60616b in frame 60596.

## Complete official library (2026-09-28)

Any official LDraw part now resolves, not just the curated 214. Pack `public/libraries/ldraw-full-2026-09-28` is built by `npm run library:full` (`scripts/build-full-library.ts`) from the official `complete.zip`, pinned by SHA-256 (`d2a69586…`, 145 MB, latest update 2026-08) in `scripts/full-library.json`, and validated end to end by `npm run library:validate`.

- **Contents.** 36,805 files, unmodified: 24,735 parts, 9,235 subparts, 2,835 primitives (including `8/` and `48/`), `LDConfig.ldr` (identical to the curated pack's), `CAreadme.txt`, `CAlicense4.txt`, `CAlicense.txt` and `NOTICE.txt`. Every file passes the same licence (CC BY 4.0) and official-provenance gates as the curated pack, and keeps its own author and licence header. Textures (`!TEXMAP` PNGs) and sample models are not packed. The archive itself references four files it does not contain (from `2374b`, `5241` and `6218741g`); these are recorded in the manifest, and those parts have unknown bounds and report an unresolved dependency when rendered. One curated file (`87552.dat`) was fetched individually from the library website and differs from the archive in header metadata only; the curated copy wins, and validation checks that the geometry is identical.
- **Format.** 3,122 content-addressed chunks (`chunks/<sha256>.bin`, the gzip of their files' exact bytes concatenated): 86 MB stored, 501 MB raw, about 28 kB per chunk. `index.json` (2.5 MB, about 620 kB compressed) lists each chunk's hash, size, file names and lengths and, for every top-level part, the chunks of its whole dependency closure and its conservative bounds. `catalog.json` (3.2 MB, about 640 kB compressed) lists number, title, category and keywords for search. Primitives are grouped by how many parts use them, so hot shared primitives sit in a few chunks; each subpart travels with the first part that uses it; parts are packed in natural order so families share chunks. Chunk boundaries are content defined, so a later release changes only neighbouring chunks. The build is deterministic (same archive, same manifest hash).
- **Trust and locks.** New projects record `library.full = { releaseId, manifestSha256 }` alongside the curated lock. The manifest pins index, catalogue and colour file; the index pins every chunk. Projects saved before this pack are re-pinned on load (it only resolves previously missing parts), with the old lock kept in `metadata.previousLocks`; a project pinned to a different complete pack keeps its pin and its extra parts stay unresolved.
- **Loading.** The curated pack still loads eagerly as one bundle. When a model (import, saved project, share link, placement) references anything else, the app loads the index once, then fetches exactly the chunks listed for the referenced parts' closures in one batch (12 in flight), verifies each against the index, and decompresses and registers the files. The import worker loads the same index so import diagnostics are exact. The fixture's nine non-catalogue parts need 20 chunks (0.49 MB). For about 200 distinct non-catalogue architectural parts, simulated on random selections, a model needs about 300 chunk requests and 7–8 MB (plus the index); 50 parts need about 110 requests and 2.7 MB.
- **Offline.** Every verified file (manifest, index, catalogue, chunks) is stored in Cache Storage (`brick-editor-ldraw-full:<release>`), and cached bytes are verified again when read. The offline app snapshot does not precache the pack. After one online load, reloading offline renders the same model. A part never loaded before is reported as unavailable offline.
- **Tampering.** A chunk (or manifest, or index) whose bytes do not match is rejected, never cached, and a corrupt cached entry is discarded and fetched again. A part whose closure touches a refused chunk resolves as missing (diagnostic box, missing-definitions health warning, strict readiness fails) instead of rendering partial geometry.
- **Bounds and queries.** Build-time bounds (the same rules as `sourceBounds`, rounded outwards to 0.001 LDU) are installed into the shared installed-bounds table when the index registers, so stacking, health boxes, explode, floors, inspector sizes and spatial queries cover the new parts without fetching geometry. Subparts or primitives referenced directly by a model have no stored bounds and stay unknown. Non-catalogue parts now have derived connectors and occupancy and, where the LDraw file names one, a derived marketplace mapping (see the 2026-09-29 section below).
- **CLI.** `brick-cli` registers the pack from disk (verified against the lock) before any operation, so validate, health, query, floors and inventory resolve every official part offline; `render`, `play` and `instructions` use the local server over the same files. Portable exports with `--include-official` take files outside the curated pack from verified chunks.
- **Parts picker.** With a search typed, an "All LDraw parts" line offers **Search every official LDraw part**. Choosing it loads the part list once and shows up to 60 ranked matches (moved-to redirects hidden, curated parts excluded because they are listed above). Cards show a rendering from the complete library's thumbnail atlas (see "Complete-library thumbnails and picker"). Choosing a card loads the part's verified geometry and derives its placement footprint, stud-grid phase and height from the stored bounds. Nothing of the pack is fetched until a model or search needs it.
- **Tests.** Unit: resolution from disk, bounds, spatial queries, lock adoption, the one-batch plan and request count, closure completeness, offline reload from a cache, and tamper rejection for chunks, cache entries and manifest. CLI: health and query on `fixtures/ldraw/full-library.ldr` (two doors including the `3861` moved-to redirect, a dome, a round-top cylinder, two fences, an arch, shutter parts), and a strict headless render. Browser: strict import and render, offline reload with the service worker installed, tamper rejection, and the picker scope. Play: auto-door detection covers the fixture's 3861 (via 3861c) and 60616b, and `brick-cli play` hinges a 60616b leaf in the door room, stops the explorer while closed and lets it through with `--open-doors`. `60616.dat` is not an official file (the official doors are `60616a`, which is in the curated pack, and `60616b`); `3861.dat` is the official redirect to `3861c.dat` and resolves through it.
- **Limits.** The renderer's variant budget is now 2,048 on desktop and 768 on phones (was 128); see "Large real-parts models". Deployment: about 4,000 files in `dist`, largest 7.6 MB; `npm run deploy:check` enforces the Cloudflare Pages limits (20,000 files, 25 MiB per file) in CI.

## Complete-library thumbnails and picker (2026-09-29)

- **What.** Every placeable part of the complete library outside the curated catalogue (23,343: every top-level part except moved-to redirects, the 224 curated parts and the 8 parts without bounds) has a rendered thumbnail; none is left as an outline. Subparts and primitives are never listed in the picker (the part list only contains top-level parts).
- **How it is rendered.** `npm run library:part-thumbnails` (`scripts/build-part-thumbnails.ts`) uses the app's own renderer in headless Chromium (SwiftShader) with the curated thumbnails' view and quality controls. Instead of one render per part, each import places 256 parts on a 16 × 16 orthographic grid, every part scaled uniformly so its box fills its cell exactly as the curated framing would; one 2560 px render is downscaled to 80 px cells. Stud-dense parts of 10 studs or more are drawn without edge lines, as for curated baseplates. 15,424 parts whose geometry has fixed colours (prints, stickers, assemblies such as wheels with tyres) are recoloured black and rendered again: pixels that do not change are print, and the difference becomes a tint mask. Bodies are white, so the UI tints them to the held colour like the curated thumbnails, while prints and fixed-colour pieces keep their own colours (15,289 cells have a visible print).
- **Pack.** `public/thumbnails/ldraw-full-2026-09-28/`: 365 WebP sheets of 64 cells (8 × 8, 640 × 640 px, about 60 kB each), 361 matching mask sheets, and `index.json` (433 kB; part → sheet and cell, printed cells). Sheets group parts by LDraw category and number, so browsing a category touches few sheets. 727 files, 24.9 MB in all. Sheets are content-addressed (`sheets/<sha256>.webp`), the index pins every sheet by hash and size and records the complete library it was rendered from, and `src/catalog/part-thumbnails-lock.json` pins the index. `npm run library:validate` checks the lock, every hash, size and sheet dimension, that every placeable part has exactly one cell (or is listed as unrendered; none are), and that nothing else is in the directory. Packing is deterministic (re-packing gives the same index hash).
- **Build time.** 92 render batches took about 80 minutes on the shared 4-core VM (two browser pages, load average around 13 from other work): 30–90 s for a plain batch, 60–250 s for a batch with prints (two renders). The render phase is resumable (one cache file per batch under `.cache/`), and a batch that fails is split in halves until the failing parts are isolated. Packing takes about 3 minutes. The sheets are committed like the complete pack; CI validates but never renders. Each scene gets a freshly loaded page: a second large import into the same page can fail to resolve official files (a renderer bug, recorded in TODO).
- **Loading and offline.** Nothing is fetched until a complete-library card is on screen. The browser then loads the index (verified against the lock) and only the sheets of cards near the screen (an IntersectionObserver per card), verifies each against its hash and keeps it in Cache Storage (`brick-editor-part-thumbnails:<pack>`), so thumbnails seen once show offline. The offline app snapshot does not precache the pack. A sheet that fails verification or is unavailable offline leaves its cards with the neutral outline.
- **Picker.** A dashed **All LDraw parts** chip next to Favourites switches the picker to the complete library: the chip row then lists LDraw categories by size (Minifig, Sticker, Tile, Technic, Brick, …) with **Catalogue** to go back, and the search box searches within the chosen category. Results come in pages of 60 cards, with the next page loaded when the end of the list scrolls into view (or with **Show more**). The existing **Search every official LDraw part** line under a catalogue search uses the same list. On phones it is the same sheet and chip row; the HUD overlap test covers it at 360 × 600 and 411 × 685.
- **Print variants.** Printed and sticker variants (`3001p01`, `973p1a`, `3626bpa1`, `10202d01`, …; 10,529 of them under 1,347 base parts, following moved-to redirects) are folded into one card per base part, showing the best-ranked member and a **+N** button. It opens the variants (search matches first, then the rest) with **All results** to go back. A search that only matches prints still finds them, on the card of their base.
- **Glass and colours.** Thumbnails follow the held colour. A transparent colour (Clear) now shows any part as glass, curated or not; curated glass is rendered opaque white so a solid colour shows it solid (the old renders were clear glass, so a pane in red looked like faint pink glass).
- **Not done.** Cells are 80 px (the curated ones are 128 px) to keep the pack near 25 MB, so they are soft on high-density phone screens. A new complete-library release (`library:update`) needs a new render (validation fails until the atlas matches the new release).

## Play figure, smooth movement and mouse look (2026-09-29)

User feedback: the mouse had to drag to look, the third-person character did not look like a brick figure, walking diagonally jittered badly (motion sickness), limbs never moved, and "collision unavailable" appeared when trying to walk.

- **Diagonal jitter, root causes and fixes.** (1) Every grounded controller sweep included gravity's small downward step, so it caught the internal edges between coplanar triangles (brick tops, plate grids) and stalled for a whole tick every few steps; walking diagonally crosses those edges constantly (measured stop-start of 1.67 → 0.06 LDU per tick). Grounded walking now asks Rapier only for horizontal travel and relies on snap-to-ground; standing still and airborne motion still sweep with gravity. (2) Each step-up or ground snap was turned into upward velocity, so climbing onto a stud launched a small hop (up to 16 LDU) that repeated all the way across a baseplate; on the ground the vertical speed now restarts from zero. (3) The capsule's round base still dips into the gaps between studs, which lie on the grid diagonals (up to 4 LDU); the camera and the figure now follow the feet through critically damped "ride" springs (the camera may lag a step, the figure at most a stud), while snapshots and collision keep the exact feet. (4) The figure was drawn at the last fixed tick while the camera was interpolated, so it juddered a whole tick (1.7 LDU) against the view; both now use the same interpolation factor. (5) The figure's heading snapped to the corrected travel direction every tick (up to 0.74 rad per tick against a wall); it now turns with a critically damped spring.
- **Stairs.** Risers up to one brick (24 LDU, a third of the figure's height, as in Minecraft) are stepped up by a swept lift-move-settle probe (Rapier's own autostep overshoots at that height); the camera rises smoothly. Taller risers (for example 32 LDU garden stairs) still need a jump (apex about 37 LDU). The profile's `stepHeight` is now 24.
- **Figure.** An original procedural toy figure built from primitives in the 72 LDU collider's proportions: cylindrical head with a top stud and a simple drawn face, tapered torso, bent arms ending in C-shaped hands, hip block and two legs. Five rigid joints turn about declared pivots (neck yaw/pitch, shoulders and hips about X); about a dozen draw calls, no download. Official LDraw minifigure parts were considered: they exist in the complete pack, but a real minifigure is about 100 LDU tall (it would have to be shrunk to the collider), loading them costs the 2.5 MB index plus chunks for builds that never needed the complete pack, and they are unavailable offline until cached.
- **Animation.** Minecraft's humanoid pattern: legs swing opposite at `cos(phase) × 1.4 × amount`, arms opposite their legs at `cos(phase) × amount`; the swing amount blends toward speed each tick (walking about 0.53, running 0.9); the phase advances with distance actually travelled. Subtle idle arm sway; the head follows the view (50° yaw either side, bounded pitch) and the body turns with it beyond that; walking backward faces forward. Airborne: arms up and forward, more when falling; flying: legs trailing with a slow flutter, a gentle bob and no stride unless skimming the ground.
- **Mouse look.** On a computer, clicking the view (or Enter Play / Resume) captures the pointer; raw mouse movement turns the view (0.0025 rad per count × sensitivity, pitch limits unchanged). Esc releases the pointer and pauses, so every button is reachable. A small "Click to look around · Esc to release" hint sits under the crosshair until the pointer is captured. Where the browser refuses pointer lock (iframes, automation), dragging still looks around. Sensitivity (0.1–5×) and invert-Y live with the Play keyboard settings and are saved per device. Touch drag-to-look is unchanged.
- **"Collision unavailable".** Not reproduced on the live site. The plausible causes are missing parts (one unresolved part turns walking off for the whole world, for example a complete-library download that failed or was interrupted) and worlds over the collision budget. Entering Play now retries loading any complete-library parts the build references and re-renders before collecting collision, and every refusal names the reason (the missing part names, or the triangle budget) instead of a generic message; a world that opens in Fly says why straight away.
- **Tests.** Unit: limb-swing maths, heading smoothing and policy, interpolation, a jitter metric on a triangulated plate, along a wall and over studs, stairs of 8/24/32 LDU, look settings and the collision explanations. ## Frame cost on large real-parts models (2026-09-29)

Batches are classified once per model update and then refilled in place: instruction steps, dimming, layer and floor ghosting, floor focus, explode and section cuts rewrite instance arrays instead of rebuilding, and ghosted, dimmed and transparent (glass) parts are instanced instead of one draw each. On the 20,000-part village a dimmed instruction step draws 1,767 calls instead of 48,468 and changes in tens of milliseconds instead of seconds; the still whole-model view draws 893 calls instead of 2,192. Above a per-profile size (desktop 4 M, phone 500 k scene triangles) frames drawn while the view moves (orbit, zoom, transform drags, Play) leave out edge lines, and phones draw at 1.25× density, restored 180 ms after the view stops; captures never reduce. The Realistic look reuses its shadow map for camera-only Play frames. Compiled part geometry is indexed (2.9× fewer vertices in the village), and picking uses a distance-ordered box broadphase over triangle meshes (about 4× faster). Physical-phone frame rates remain unmeasured. See [rendering](RENDERING.md#frame-cost-on-large-models) and [verification](VERIFICATION.md#frame-cost-on-large-real-parts-models-29-september-2026).

- **Limits.** Opening a few-hundred-part template used to compile its parts on the main thread (about 10 s for the house on the test VM, page unresponsive); see "Fast, non-blocking model loading" below. ## Fast, non-blocking model loading (2026-09-29)

Opening the house template froze the page for about 16 s on the test VM. It now responds throughout: parts appear about 0.8 s after choosing it, it is complete in about 3 s, and after a reload it reopens in about 0.5 s without compiling anything.

- **Cause.** 95% of the time was one function: the pinned LDrawLoader's normal smoothing was quadratic in a part's size (seconds for the 32 × 32 baseplate). The loader is vendored with that loop made linear; its output is unchanged bit for bit.
- **Compile workers.** Official part geometry compiles in two to four workers (at most two on phones) and comes back as one transferable buffer per part; the main thread wraps it in Three objects with shared colour materials. Custom parts, raw primitives and parts with file-local colours still compile on the main thread, and everything falls back to it if workers cannot run.
- **Geometry cache.** Compiled parts are kept in IndexedDB, keyed by compiler version, both library manifests, render context and part; every entry is SHA-256 verified on read, the cache is bounded (128 MB desktop, 48 MB phone) with least-recently-used eviction, and quota or storage failures only mean compiling again.
- **Short tasks, progressive drawing.** Remaining main-thread loading work ends its task every ~12 ms. A newly opened model is drawn as its parts compile (most-used parts first, redraws spaced by their cost and by the GPU keeping up), the camera frames the first parts, and a thin progress bar with a percentage shows in the status toast (it stays visible while loading on phones). Edits of the open model still swap in at once.
- **Large models.** The workspace derives the occurrence list once per project (not on every render) and shares it with the rig and layer panels; the renderer and autosave no longer deep-copy each new project again; handle cloning is cheaper. The 20,000-part village shows its first parts ~40% sooner with 30–55% less main-thread blocking.
- **Roadster "Unsaved changes".** No revision was created on load: the save indicator said "Unsaved changes" for every newly opened project until autosave finished (hidden before by the house's freeze, visible for the quick-loading roadster). A project that is unchanged since it was opened now reads "No changes" (then "Saving…"/"Saved" as autosave writes it for recovery).
- **API.** `render.compileStats()` reports worker compiles, cache hits and the cache's size; `?compileWorkers=0|N`, `?geometryCache=0` and `?progressive=0` are diagnostic switches.
- **Tests.** Unit: `part-compile.test.ts` (vendored vs upstream loader, record round trip, transferable views, local colours, missing dependencies, malformed records), `geometry-cache.test.ts` (hash verification, LRU eviction and restore, quota and broken storage), `load-scheduling.test.ts` (time slicing, yielding, the worker pool's concurrency, lazy sources, cache use and fallbacks). Browser: `load-performance.spec.ts` (house in workers, progressive, progress bar, split into tasks, cache after reload, corrupt entry recompiled) and the template chooser now checks the save state.
- **Reopening the page with a large saved project.** The saved copy is verified in a worker and handed to the editor without a second validation or copy, occurrence expansion is about 2.5× faster, and the model loads through the same short tasks and progressive drawing. Recovering a 20,000-part project: longest task 1.2–1.9 s → 0.45–0.68 s, a mode switch clicked meanwhile answered in 2.5 s instead of 5–10 s; a 2,600-part build: longest task 1.1 s → 0.18 s. An import started while recovery is still running wins, as before (recovery stands down). Tests: `startup-recovery.spec.ts` (reload with a 20,000-part autosave: mode switch answered before the model finishes, longest task bound, every part drawn); `storage.spec.ts` now delays recovery by holding the restore worker's reply.
- **Complete-library fix.** A part loaded only as a chunk neighbour of another part counted as loaded without its dependencies, so importing it later failed ("Unresolved dependency: 2313b.dat" after a large import); its closure is now completed.
- **Remaining.** On the test VM the longest task while opening a template is the first frame's synchronous shader compilation (0.3–1.3 s in software WebGL). Very large models still block for up to about 1–2 s in document replacement and the first workspace derivation, and in autosave serialization. See [verification](VERIFICATION.md#model-loading-compile-off-the-main-thread-29-september-2026) and [architecture ADR 009](ARCHITECTURE.md#adr-009--off-main-thread-part-compilation-and-the-geometry-cache).

## Play feedback: driving, seats and the orbit camera (2026-09-29)

- **Jeep work-budget error.** Steering and driving the Off-road jeep together stopped it within seconds with "Body transfer exceeds collision work budget". Root cause: the seated rider's body sweep read its yaw back from the pelvis frame, which wraps at ±180°; turning through due south asked for a 358° sweep in one tick (over a thousand 5 mm angular segments against a limit of 128). Every vehicle and rider sweep now uses the short turn from its start pose (`continuousPoses`), a turn needing more than one query's segments is split into up to 16 adaptive substeps, and a move that would still exceed a budget holds its pose for that tick only (input kept, no stop reason, retried next tick) instead of reporting a user-visible stop. See [protected driving](PLAY-VEHICLES.md).
- **Sweep broadphase.** The BVH query skips pure support (triangles and whole branches whose top is no higher than every box's bottom throughout the move), so floors, studded plates and ground under a vehicle are never candidates; the remaining candidates (up to 4,096, was 512) form one transient triangle mesh, so a sweep costs `segments × boxes × 2` queries whatever the triangle count, and a contact still names the nearest source triangle.
- **Roadster driver seat.** The Roadster car is rebuilt to minifigure proportions (58 parts, still four wide and all catalogue parts): wheelbase 8 studs, the Minifig Seat 2 × 2 two studs behind the steering wheel with an open row behind its backrest, and two-plate sills so the seated hips fit between them and the arms rest over them. Its rig has a `driverSeat` (hips 18 LDU above the cushion, approach and exits 90 LDU out on either side); entry, driving and exit pass Play's full clearance checks. Build check: no overlaps, on grid, one connected group.
- **Seated head.** The seated head turns with the look (±0.7 rad) and nods forward with it (to −0.35 rad), never tipping back into a backrest; the torso/head collision box contains every such pose (unit-tested against the figure's geometry).
- **Third-person orbit camera.** The camera yaw is the player's look, independent of the figure. Movement is relative to the camera (W away from it, S towards it, A/D strafe); the figure turns smoothly (the same critically damped spring) to face its movement, so walking back towards the camera turns it round, and keeps its heading when standing still, so dragging the view orbits 360° round it and shows the face. The head follows the look within 50° and eases back to straight ahead as the camera swings to the front. First person is unchanged (the body follows the view; walking backward faces forward). The seated chase camera orbits the vehicle the same way. The camera-collision arm, the ride smoothing and the jitter tests are unchanged. The API keeps its fields: `yaw`/`pitch` are the look (camera) and `avatar.heading` the body; the snapshot schema now describes both.
- **Tests.** Unit: `play-vehicle-steer.test.ts` (short-turn poses, adaptive substeps, a budget holding for one tick, a seated driver circling through due south), `play-vehicle-obstacles.test.ts` (support skipped, merged narrowphase, candidate limit), `play-orbit.test.ts` (orbit maths, camera-relative movement at four camera yaws, turn-to-face without jitter or overshoot, 360° orbit keeping the heading and showing the face, first person unchanged, seated head and chase-camera orbit), `template-samples.test.ts` (the jeep circling for 15 s seated and remote with no stop; the roadster's seat entry, drive, turn and exit), `play-seated-profile.test.ts` (head pitch range inside the body box). Browser: `play-orbit.spec.ts` (the jeep driving and turning with W+A for 15 s through ±180° without an error; the roadster's Enter driver seat, drive and Exit vehicle; third person walking back turns the figure, dragging orbits the camera and the front view shows the face).

## Photo look: path-traced stills (2026-09-29)

User feedback from a real phone: "Realistic look is fine but Photo mode isn't very different." Photo now path-traces still views with three-gpu-pathtracer (MIT, bundled, loaded on first use): global illumination and colour bleeding, refractive transparent parts, clear-coated ABS, soft studio-softbox shadows, depth of field focused on the tapped part (else the model's centre), a seamless studio sweep, PBR Neutral tone mapping and a film grade. Any motion draws realistic raster frames; tracing starts 220 ms after the view comes to rest, refines in tiles adapted to the frame rate, shows "Refining photo… n%" in the toast and stops drawing when done (desktop 256 samples, phones 64 in 3×3 tiles). Without float render targets, above the triangle budget (desktop 2 M, phone 600 k; every template fits), with a section cut or in Play, Photo falls back to the accumulated raster photo and says why. ## Snap together: connected building (2026-09-29)

User question: is there a mode where parts can only go where they attach, or on the ground or a baseplate? There was not (Place snapped to verified fits when it found one but otherwise placed anywhere, floating included). Now there is, on by default.

- **Setting.** **Snap together**, a switch next to the part name on the placement card (and a checkbox in Inspector → Move and rotate). On for new and existing users; turning it off is remembered per device (`localStorage`). A tool setting, not document data: opening or editing imported models (Santorini, templates) is unaffected.
- **Rule** (`src/edit/connected-placement.ts`, [Connected building](CONNECTORS.md#connected-building-snap-together)). A part holds when it clashes with nothing and either a verified connector mates (studs/anti-studs above or below, side studs, jumpers, hinge pins in a frame), or its lowest point is on the ground (y = 0; a baseplate there holds, and parts on it hold through its studs), or, only where connector data is missing on either side, its underside rests on another part's top (reported as an unchecked connection). Otherwise it floats and is refused. Two verified parts that only touch do not hold.
- **Place.** Fits still come first (all of them connect); a proposal with no fit is checked. A refused preview is shown with a red tint, the card reads “Nothing to connect to here — move it onto studs or the ground.” (or “Another part is in the way here…”) in place of the tap hint, the toast repeats it and **Place part** is disabled. Rotate, Next fit and typed coordinates are checked the same way.
- **Moves.** Move and rotate handles, exact move/rotation and the Inspector's Rotate 90° and 1 plate up/down check the moved parts as a group against the rest: refused only when the group held before and would float or clash after (a handle drag springs back, “Not moved: it would float…”). Parts that already floated move freely; parts left behind are not checked.
- **Not enforced:** paste, duplicate, arrays, fill, typed Inspector positions, undo/redo, imports, and all automation commands. Automation asks with `connectors.validatePlacement({ part, position, angle? | basis? })` and `connectors.validateMove({ transforms })`; `capabilities().connectors.connectedBuilding` describes the setting ([API](API.md#connectors)).
- **Tests.** Unit `tests/unit/connected-placement.test.ts`: ground and floating, stud holds (on top, hanging under an overhang), half-stud offset refused, baseplate on the ground and bricks on its studs, clash refusal, door in its frame by hinge pins, unverified parts resting on a top (and not beside or in the air), a verified part on an unverified one, group moves (lifted stack refused, slide along the ground kept, moving onto another brick's studs) and already-floating parts moving freely. Browser `tests/browser/snap-together.spec.ts`: on by default; a floating tap refused with the message, disabled Place and `validatePlacement` agreeing; placing on studs, on the ground, on a baseplate and a door into its frame; switched off, free placement and the setting remembered after reload; exact move lifting a stack refused (`validateMove` agreeing), a slide applied, and the lift allowed with the setting off; a handle drag that would float springs back; phone card with the wrapped refusal and every control on screen.
- **Limits.** The ground is y = 0 only (an upper floor's workplane is not ground; parts there hold on the parts below). Unverified families (clips, brick hinges, brackets, Technic) can only rest on tops or stand on the ground. Support and clutch strength are not modelled: one stud holds a whole part.

## Navigate by default; box and lasso selection in 3D (2026-09-29)

- **Navigate first.** The editor opens in Navigate on phones and desktop, and every newly opened build (template, file, project) returns to it; Escape from Place, Paint or Measure goes back to Navigate, and the Place card's Cancel does too. Select stays one key in the tool column/dock (and V). Selection tools' match, all and connected buttons hold Select for you, since transform handles need it. In the editing tools right-drag now orbits (Shift+right-drag pans); Navigate keeps left-orbit, right-pan.
- **What went wrong in 3D** (measured on House with garden, the castle, the café and a generated 20,000-part village; software WebGL, 1440 × 1000):
  - Box/lasso sat three levels deep (Inspector → Selection tools → Gesture), and while it was on, a tap ran a zero-area region that cleared the selection instead of selecting the tapped part.
  - Through tested every triangle: 0.1–0.5 s on the house and café, and on the village a small box failed after 8.2 s with "exceeds five million triangles". It also used "any triangle touches", so a box on the front wall took the baseplate, long bricks and floor plates that merely crossed it (58 parts for a box that centred on 32).
  - Through ignored the section cut: parts wholly cut away were still selected. Ghosted floors and layers were drawn as solid occluders in Visible and selected like visible parts.
  - Visible drew one proxy mesh per drawable (about 20,000 draws on the village, 4.0 s per selection) and set the ID target's viewport through the canvas pixel ratio, so on screens with a density above 1 (every phone) the ID image was scaled against the region.
  - A single pixel counted: a region grazing a part's edge, or a sliver seen through a gap, took it.
  - Nothing showed what would be selected until release; Add/Remove lived only in a drop-down.
- **Visible** (default): only parts you can see. One ID pass renders the render batches with an ID shader (instanced draws use their own instance buffers and `gl_InstanceID`; merged raw batches carry a per-vertex entry index; ~300 draws on the village instead of ~20,000), depth tested, clipped by the section plane, at CSS-pixel size whatever the pixel ratio. Ghosted parts neither occlude nor count. Rule default **touching**: at least 4 seen pixels inside the region (or all of a smaller part's). **Centre inside** and **Fully inside** (every seen pixel inside and none at the view's edge) are the alternatives.
- **Through**: everything inside regardless of depth, from each shown part's box projected to a hull (clipped at the camera plane, so parts crossing it keep an honest outline). Rule default **centre inside**; **touching** (outline overlaps) and **fully inside** are the alternatives. Parts wholly beyond a section cut are skipped.
- **Always excluded**: hidden layers, floors above a focused floor, instruction-hidden parts, cut-away parts; locked and out-of-scope layers are left out by the editing scope as before. **Only the focused floor** (on by default) also leaves out the floors below a focused floor, so one box takes a whole floor's walls.
- **Gestures.** Desktop: in Select, a left drag of more than 6 px draws a box (a still click still selects), L switches box/lasso, Shift adds, Alt (or Ctrl/Cmd) removes, and modifiers can change mid-drag; Escape cancels. Touch: Inspector → Selection tools → **Box or lasso select** puts the sheet away and shows a chip (Box/Lasso, Visible/Through, New/Add/Remove, Done); one finger draws, a still tap still selects, a second finger cancels the region and pans/zooms. The view is captured once when a region starts; every move re-evaluates it, outlines the parts it would take (orange, red for removal) in one line draw and shows a count pill ("12 parts", "+ 12 parts", "− 12 parts").
- **Performance** (village of 20,000 parts, 300 variants, 10.7 M triangles in view, software WebGL on the shared test VM): the Visible ID pass is 300 draws instead of about 20,000 and costs a fraction of an ordinary frame of the same view: 2.3–4.8 s against 15.8 s on a quiet VM, 12–19 s against 120–220 s under heavy load (the first full draw after loading pays SwiftShader's warm-up either way). Old per-part proxies took 4.0 s per selection on the quiet VM. Each pointer move then re-evaluates the captured image in 34–60 ms for a 30 % box. Through: snapshot 0.2–0.9 s, per move 11–17 ms, a one-shot 5 % box 0.1–0.2 s (it failed with the triangle budget before). House and café: Visible 0.3–0.6 s per gesture (as before; raster bound on this VM), Through under 10 ms (was up to 0.5 s). On a hardware GPU the ID pass is one frame of batched draws.
- **Tests.** Unit `tests/unit/region-selection.test.ts`: outlines and camera-plane clipping, touching/centre/fully-inside for both modes, the pixel threshold, view-edge rule, concave lasso gaps, hulls, modifiers and count labels. Browser `selection.spec.ts`: Navigate on load and after opening a build (1440 × 1000 and 411 × 686); desktop box, click, Through, Alt-remove, Shift-add lasso via L, live counts and Escape; density-2 screens; hidden and cut-away parts neither occlude nor get selected; the house's front wall with Visible versus both walls with Through; touch box mode at 411 × 686 and 360 × 800 (draw, Add, Remove, two-finger navigation, Done); 20,000-part timing and draw count. `hud-layout.spec.ts` checks the box select chip for overlaps on phones.
- **Limits.** Through judges parts by their box, so a slope or a diagonal part counts from its box corners. A part smaller than a CSS pixel may leave no pixel in the ID image. The snapshot is taken when the drag starts, so a model changing under the drag cancels it.

## Backdrops and a dynamic-physics sample (2026-09-30)

User feedback: “Add some more backgrounds … when driving the jeep you can't tell if you are moving because the background is all blank”, and “Do any existing scenes display dynamic physics?” (none did: the physics playground had become a hidden test fixture).

- **Backdrops.** Six presets: Blank (the original light background and grid, still the default), Meadow, Street map (a toy play mat with roads, zebra crossings, a park, a car park, houses and a sports field), Beach (sand, a foam line and the sea), Night city (the street map under lamp light, stars, a lit skyline) and Studio (a neutral sweep). Each is a sky dome, an endless textured ground that fades into the horizon colour, and horizon silhouettes (hills and trees, a toy town, islands and boats, a skyline). All textures are drawn procedurally on a canvas at runtime from fixed seeds (no image assets, nothing fetched, reproducible captures): tiles of 128² to 1024² plus a 2048 × 256 horizon band (1024 × 128 on phones). See [rendering](RENDERING.md#backdrops).
- **Saved with the build.** `project.scene.backdrop`, set by the undoable `scene.set` command, round-trips through native projects and autosave, and is left out of LDraw export and clipboard fragments. The samples open on fitting backdrops: jeep, roadster and café on the street map; house, castle, windmill farm and playground on the meadow; the lighthouse on the beach.
- **Where.** The editor viewport, Play (same renderer) and captures. **Camera views → Look → Backdrop** shows six swatches and a **Grid** checkbox (the grid overlay is a per-viewer preference and turns translucent on textured grounds). Automation: `render.backdrop.get()/set({name?, grid?})`, `render.image({backdrop?})` with `manifest.backdrop: {name, drawn}`, CLI `--backdrop` for `render`, `render-collection` and `play`.
- **Looks and Play.** The environment is its own module (`src/render/environment.ts`) and adds no lights: its ground is a lit, shadow-receiving Lambert surface, so Realistic/Photo shadows and AO land on it and the shadow-catcher plane hides while a backdrop draws its own ground; sky and horizon are drawn into the same HDR frame and tone-mapped with it. The path-traced Photo look traces the backdrop's ground (flagged `photoStage`, with a plain material of its average colour) instead of its studio sweep. Play keeps its session-only collision ground at Y = 0; the visible ground sits 0.6 LDU below.
- **Cost.** +3 draw calls and about 2,560 triangles; 0.09–6.3 MB of textures on phones; 12–120 ms to draw the textures when a backdrop is chosen. On the VM's software WebGL, main-thread submission per Play frame went from 3.0 ms to 4.5–6.5 ms (phone profile, jeep driving); full-screen texture shading is far slower on SwiftShader than on phone GPUs. Found and fixed on the way: a ground drawn as one fan of huge triangles was near-clipped with broken depth in first-person Play, so the ground is a ring mesh; three's `cameraPosition` uniform is not fed to Lambert materials, so the fade uses its own uniform.
- **Playground park** (new sample, 110 parts): loose crates and barrels on a tiled plaza, a see-saw, a roundabout, a swing, a ramp, trees and a fence. Nine rigs (six loose single-group rigs, three revolute joints; 12 bodies) set the new `dynamics.startDynamic`, so the Play card opens with **Mechanism physics · Dynamic** chosen; the project's one-line Play hint (`scene.playHint`, “Push the crates and barrels, then swing!”) shows on the card and for six seconds after entering Play. Physics settings gained a **Start Play with dynamic physics** checkbox. Rig groups explicitly marked loose (`anchored: false`) are left out of the build check's group count and Model health's connection and separate-group checks, which report them as loose objects. See [templates](TEMPLATES.md) and [Play physics](PLAY-PHYSICS.md).
- **Tests.** Unit `tests/unit/scene-backdrop.test.ts` (preset table, `scene.set` undo/redo and refusals, native round trip, no LDraw/fragment leak, sample defaults) and new cases in `template-samples.test.ts` (playground grid/overlaps/one group/health, rigs within limits, and in dynamic Play the explorer pushes crate 3 across the plaza and swings the seat; kinematic Play leaves the crate put). Browser `tests/browser/backdrop.spec.ts`: switching in the views popover, grid toggle, saved and restored after reload; captures differ by backdrop and record it, per-capture override, transparent captures draw nothing; the jeep drives on the street map in Play with the backdrop in the capture manifest; a path-traced Photo capture over the meadow traces its green ground instead of the white studio sweep; the playground opens Play in Dynamic with its hint and a walk pushes a crate. `templates.spec.ts` lists the new card.
