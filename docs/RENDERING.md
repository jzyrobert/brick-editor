# Rendering looks

The viewport and captures offer three shading looks. The look is a separate setting from the render quality profile (`fast`/`balanced`/`photo`, which controls edges, shadow map size, pixel-ratio cap, tone mapping and exposure). A look never changes the document, the geometry or the stored quality profile.

| Look                 | What it draws                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Where                               |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Standard** default | The original look: hemisphere + sun + fill lights, LDraw edge and conditional lines, `MeshStandardMaterial` values from LDrawLoader. Unchanged, drawn directly to the canvas. Optional [soft outlines](#soft-outlines-standard).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Everywhere; the capture default     |
| **Realistic**        | The [studio shared with Photo](#one-studio-for-realistic-and-photo) as image-based light plus its key light, tuned ABS/finish materials, soft shadows fitted to the model on a shadow-catcher ground, ambient occlusion, no outlines, PBR Neutral tone mapping, a light vignette.                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Interactive and captures            |
| **Photo**            | A path-traced studio photograph of a still view: global illumination and colour bleeding, refractive transparent parts, glossy ABS with a clear coat, soft area-light shadows from studio softboxes, depth of field focused on the tapped part, on a seamless studio sweep, with PBR Neutral tone mapping and a film grade. Refines progressively while the view is still; every moving frame is a realistic raster frame. Falls back to accumulated realistic frames where path tracing is unavailable. | Interactive (when still) and stills |

Choose a look in **Camera views → Look**. The choice is stored per viewer (`localStorage` key `brick-editor-render-look`, ignored when storage is unavailable). Photo-mode captures use the chosen look. Automation: `render.look.get()`, `render.look.set(name, controls?)`, and `render.image({ …, look?, lookControls? })`, which defaults to `standard` so existing captures stay byte-identical. The capture manifest records the resolved `look` and whether the output was tone-mapped. The CLI takes `--look standard|realistic|photo` for `render`, `render-collection` and `play`.

`lookControls` (bounded, validated): `environment` `none|studio|room` (`room`: three's RoomEnvironment, the Realistic look before the shared studio), `materials` `ldraw|plastic`, `ambientOcclusion` `off|gtao`, `edges` `quality|hidden|soft`, `shadows` `quality|soft`, `ground` `grid|shadow`, `samples` 1–64 (raster accumulation), `vignette` 0–0.5, `toneMapping` `quality|neutral|agx`, `exposureScale` 0.25–4, `renderer` `raster|path`, `pathSamples` 1–4096, `depthOfField` 0–1, `backdrop` `none|studio|floor`, `grade` `none|film`. `render.look.photo()` reports how the last photo still was drawn (`renderer`, fallback `reason`, traced `triangles`, `samples`, BVH `buildMs`, and for path-traced stills the traced `width`/`height`, estimated `noise`, `refineMs` and `compileMs`); photo capture manifests carry the same as `photo`.

## One studio for Realistic and Photo

Realistic used to be lit by three's `RoomEnvironment` (a grey box room with ceiling lights) plus the editor's hemisphere, key and fill lights, while Photo traced its own procedural studio that turned with the camera. The two looks disagreed: the key came from different sides, Realistic's shadows fell elsewhere, and switching from a moving (raster) frame to the traced still visibly relit the model. Both looks now use one studio (`src/render/studio-lighting.ts`), built the way product-shot viewers build theirs: an HDR image of light panels on dark walls, generated procedurally on the CPU (no asset).

- **The studio.** Five panels (`STUDIO_PANELS`): a key softbox high on the left of the camera, a dim cool fill on the right, two strip lights behind for rim highlights and a soft overhead light. Each panel is a rectangle with smoothstep edges on the plane one unit along its direction, drawn into a 512×256 equirectangular image. Panels are placed in the frame of the camera `fit()` frames a model with and turned once to that camera's azimuth (`STUDIO_AZIMUTH`). The studio is fixed in the world: it no longer turns with the camera, so the raster frames Photo draws while the view moves and the traced still agree, and the raster key light that casts the cached shadow map does not move during an orbit.
- **Photo** traces the image as it is (`tracedStudio()`, generated once per page): every panel is an importance-sampled area light, and the tracer traces its own floor (the sweep or a backdrop's ground).
- **Realistic** splits the key softbox (`rasterStudioLight()`): 80 % of its irradiance (`STUDIO_KEY_SHARE`, the panel's radiance integrated over its solid angle with `panelIrradiance()`) becomes the directional key light along the softbox's direction, so it casts the fitted soft shadow where Photo's traced shadow falls, and the remaining 20 % stays in the image as the softbox's reflection in glossy plastic. The other panels light the model only through the environment. Below the horizon the raster image gets a lit floor standing in for the light the traced floor bounces: the upper hemisphere's irradiance × the floor's albedo × 0.6 / π, where the albedo is Photo's sweep on Blank and Studio, or the colour the tracer traces a backdrop's ground in (so a meadow bounces green into Realistic as it does in Photo). The image is converted to half floats (32-bit float textures do not filter linearly on many phones) and prefiltered once with `PMREMGenerator.fromEquirectangular`; maps are cached per ground colour (at most two studio maps), so switching looks or orbiting never regenerates one. The hemisphere light drops to 0.12 and the fill light is off. A backdrop's ground is a Lambert material, which image-based light does not reach, so with the studio it gets the studio's sky as unshadowed light (`rasterStudioSkyLight()`: the upper hemisphere's irradiance on an upward surface, about 55 % of what Photo's studio gives it); without that, the ground in the key light's shadow was lit by the hemisphere light alone and turned near black, where Photo's traced shadow is a soft grey.
- `lookControls.environment: "room"` keeps the previous `RoomEnvironment` lighting for comparison and for automation that depends on it.

Side-by-side captures (Realistic left, Photo at 16 samples right, 400×300 each, desktop, each template's own backdrop), before and after: [House with garden](screenshots/looks/studio-house.jpg), [Off-road jeep](screenshots/looks/studio-jeep.jpg), [Small castle](screenshots/looks/studio-castle.jpg). Before, the two looks lit the model from different directions and cast different shadows, and Realistic's shadow on the street map was black. After, both are lit by the same softbox, their shadows fall the same way, and Realistic's shadow on a backdrop is a soft grey like Photo's.

**Cost.** The studio costs nothing per frame beyond the previous room environment: the same one PMREM map sampled per pixel, one directional light with the same fitted shadow map, one fewer light (fill off). Generating it is a one-off on first use of a realistic look: the CPU image and its prefilter took 407 ms against 153 ms for `RoomEnvironment` on the loaded test VM (5,000-part village, mobile profile; the sky-light integral was then cut to a 128×64 image, saving about 100 ms); a second backdrop colour adds one more prefilter. Measured orbit frames on that build (phone 390×844 at DPR 3, mobile profile, SwiftShader, median of 8): Realistic with the studio 602 draws and 15.9 ms CPU per frame, with the room 602 draws and 16.8 ms. Wall times (12–16 s per frame on software WebGL under a load average of 20) are dominated by rasterisation and vary more between runs than between the two.

**Studio refinement (9 October 2026).** The key panel is wider (half extents
0.4 × 0.3 instead of 0.26 × 0.2), with a softer edge and lower peak radiance.
Its integrated red-channel irradiance stays at about 3.10, so broad reflections
do not raise the directional key's intensity or the look's exposure. The light
is slightly more neutral, and wider rim strips make curved surfaces easier to
read after environment-map filtering. ABS roughness is 0.28 instead of 0.24,
softening its sharp highlights while keeping its glossy finish. Both looks use
the refined studio; Photo retains its separate clear-coated physical material.
This is parameter tuning: five panels, the same cached 512 × 256 environment,
the same materials and shaders, and the same shadow maps. Phone Realistic still
draws directly with AO and vignette off. Verification and measured comparisons
are in [the verification record](VERIFICATION.md#realistic-studio-refinement--9-october-2026).

**Contact shadow and reflective floor (not kept).** The other site's realistic mode lays a blurred contact shadow and a reflective floor under the model. A contact shadow was prototyped for Realistic (the model drawn once from below into a 256² depth-alpha texture, blurred twice, on a plane under the model, redrawn only after scene changes like the cached shadow map) and removed: Realistic already has a fitted soft shadow on its shadow-catcher plane plus GTAO, so the difference in captures was faint, while the extra pass is another full draw of the model after every edit, as costly as the shadow-map pass, and the prototype misdrew with the batched draws in the viewport. A reflective floor needs the model drawn a second time, mirrored, every frame, which doubles the draw calls on the phone budget, so it was not attempted; Photo's traced sweep already carries a faint reflection.

## Soft outlines (Standard)

LDraw draws every part's outline in its colour's `EDGE` value from LDConfig, nearly black for most colours, which reads as a technical drawing on large builds. The Look menu's **Soft outlines** switch (shown only with Standard; stored per viewer under `localStorage` key `brick-editor-soft-edges`) tints each outline towards its part's body colour instead: `softEdgeColor()` keeps 35–55 % of the LDConfig edge colour (more for light bodies, so white parts keep a visible mid-grey outline) and mixes in the rest from the body colour at 72 % brightness, in sRGB, so a red brick gets a dark red outline and a black part keeps its subtle grey one. Automation: `lookControls.edges: "soft"` on the Standard look (`render.look.set("standard", { edges: "soft" })` or a capture's `lookControls`); captures without it stay byte-identical.

Cost: none per frame. LDrawLoader shares one edge material and one conditional-line material per colour, and the batches draw those shared materials, so the outlines are recoloured in place (`tuneEdges()`, restored from `userData.lookEdgeBase`): no material, shader program or draw call is added. Switching costs one walk over the loaded prototypes. Measured on the mobile profile (phone 390×844 at DPR 3, SwiftShader, orbit frames): on a 5,000-part village, 893 draws and 2.47 M lines with or without soft outlines, 24–31 ms CPU per frame either way, switching in 2–3 ms; on the 20,000-part village, 650 draws and 4.98 M lines either way, switching in 2 ms. Captures without `edges: "soft"` stay byte-identical. Screenshots: [Standard and soft outlines](screenshots/looks/soft-outlines.jpg) on the house, castle and jeep.

## Backdrops

A backdrop is the world drawn around the build: a sky, a ground and a band of horizon silhouettes. It is chosen per project, is saved in the project (`project.scene.backdrop`, native projects only; LDraw export ignores it) and shows in the editor viewport, in Play and in captures. It adds no collision: Play keeps its session-only ground plane at Y = 0, and the visible ground sits 0.6 LDU below it.

| Backdrop   | Name     | Ground                                                                                                                                                                                        | Horizon                           | Samples using it                    |
| ---------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Blank      | `blank`  | none: the original light background and grid                                                                                                                                                  | none                              | default for new builds              |
| Meadow     | `grass`  | grass blades, darker and lighter patches, daisies and buttercups (512² tile, 768 LDU) with 7,200 LDU variation                                                                                | rolling hills, tree clumps, pines | house, castle, windmill, playground |
| Street map | `street` | toy play mat: 300 LDU roads with dashed centre lines, zebra crossings, stop lines, pavements; a park with a pond, a car park with toy cars, houses and a sports field (1024² tile, 2,560 LDU) | toy town and trees                | jeep, roadster, café                |
| Beach      | `beach`  | sand with ripples and shells (256², 512 LDU), wet sand and a foam line at a wavy shore 2,600 LDU out, sea beyond (256², 1,536 LDU)                                                            | islands and sailing boats         | lighthouse                          |
| Night city | `night`  | the street map at night with warm pools of lamp light                                                                                                                                         | lit skyline and 420 stars         |                                     |
| Studio     | `studio` | faint two-tone chequer (128², 1,600 LDU)                                                                                                                                                      | none (neutral sweep)              |                                     |

Choose one in **Camera views → Look → Backdrop** (six swatches; the line under them names the backdrop). Choosing is one undoable `scene.set` command. The **Grid** checkbox next to it shows or hides the editor grid overlay (a per-viewer preference, `localStorage` key `brick-editor-grid-hidden`); on a textured ground the grid is drawn translucent. The chooser cards stay on white; each sample opens on its own backdrop.

**How it is made** (`src/render/environment.ts`, `src/render/environment-textures.ts`, data in `src/core/scene.ts`). Every texture is drawn at runtime on a 2D canvas from a fixed seed: no image assets are shipped or fetched, the site stays offline-capable and captures are reproducible. Ground tiles wrap seamlessly (features crossing an edge are drawn again on the other side). The ground is one mesh that follows the camera; its texture coordinates come from world position, so the pattern stays put and the ground is effectively endless. It is a flat disc in rings that double in radius (about 1,500 triangles), because one fan of huge triangles from under the camera was clipped with bad depth on SwiftShader. The ground is an ordinary `MeshLambertMaterial` with an `onBeforeCompile` hook: world-space tile lookup, the large-scale variation texture, the beach's shore zone, and a fade to the sky's horizon colour between 2,600 LDU and 11,000 LDU from the camera. The fade is in the ground's shader, not scene fog, so builds are never fogged. The sky is a vertex-coloured dome with no depth test, drawn first and scaled to the camera's far plane; the horizon band is an open cylinder 12,000 LDU out with an alpha texture whose foot blends into the haze colour. Orthographic views hide the sky and horizon, show the backdrop's background colour and do not fade the ground.

**With the looks.** The environment owns no lights and does not touch the look pipeline. Two things tie a backdrop to the realistic looks' [shared studio](#one-studio-for-realistic-and-photo): the raster studio's floor bounce takes the colour the path tracer traces the ground in, so a meadow bounces green light up into Realistic as it does in Photo; and the ground's shader adds the studio's sky as unshadowed light (`uSkyLight`, zero in Standard), because image-based light does not reach its Lambert material. The ground is a lit, shadow-receiving material, so Realistic shadows and ambient occlusion land on it, and the shadow-catcher plane is hidden while a backdrop draws its own ground. Sky and horizon are unlit meshes in the same HDR frame, so the pipeline's tone mapping applies to them too. For the path-traced Photo look the ground sits in its own scene group flagged `userData.photoStage`, with a `photoMaterial` of the ground's average colour (the tracer traces no textures), so Photo traces the backdrop's ground in place of its studio sweep; the sky and horizon are raster-only and are left out of both the trace and the overlay pass drawn over it (the traced image shows the backdrop's background colour behind the model). The ground mesh moves in 4,096 LDU steps (its texture coordinates are world-fixed), so orbiting a still view does not rebuild the tracer's scene.

**Captures.** `render.image({backdrop?})` draws the project's backdrop unless the request names another; over a transparent background nothing of the backdrop is drawn. The manifest records `backdrop: {name, drawn}`.

**Cost** (`.local` measurement, jeep in Play, driving, mobile resource profile, 412 × 780 at DPR 2.625, SwiftShader on the ARM64 VM): three extra draw calls (sky, ground, horizon; 171 → 174) and about 2,560 extra triangles. Texture memory with mipmaps on phones: street and night 6.3 MB, meadow 2.2 MB, beach 1.5 MB, studio 0.09 MB (desktop draws the horizon band at 2048 × 256, phones at 1024 × 128; anisotropy 4 on phones, 8 on desktop). Building the textures takes 12–120 ms once when a backdrop is chosen. Main-thread submission per Play frame rose from 3.0 ms to 4.5–6.5 ms on this VM. SwiftShader shades every pixel on the CPU, so a full-screen textured ground there costs far more than the plain clear of Blank (about 0.45 s against 2–3 s per two-frame step at that buffer size); on phone GPUs one lit, two-texture ground pass is small next to the build itself. Blank stays the default for new builds and costs nothing.

## Phones and Play

On the mobile resource profile, Realistic keeps the cheap parts (IBL, materials, fitted 1024² soft shadows, shadow ground, no outlines) and drops ambient occlusion and the vignette. It then draws directly to the canvas like Standard, with no off-screen targets. Photo on phones path-traces 64 samples (desktop 256) in 3×3 tiles with 4 bounces, and accepts up to 600,000 traced triangles (desktop 2,000,000; `photoTriangles` in `render-budget.ts`); its raster fallback uses 2× MSAA and 12 samples. Play never path-traces or accumulates samples (every Play frame moves), and phones also drop AO while playing. Play's pixel-ratio cap (`PLAY_PIXEL_RATIO_CAP`) applies to every look.

## Frame cost on large models

Large real-parts builds (10,000–30,000 parts) are drawn through render-only batches (`src/render/batching.ts`); the occurrence handles stay authoritative for picking, selection, collision and export. Measurements are in [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md#frame-cost-on-large-real-parts-models-29-september-2026) and [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md#lighter-handles-prepared-frames-and-adaptive-culling-30-september-2026).

- **Occurrence handles are records** (`src/render/occurrence-handles.ts`). A handle is the occurrence ID, its compiled prototype, a matrix (relative to the model root, explode lift included), a visibility flag and the treatments applied to it, about a tenth of a kilobyte, instead of a cloned Object3D tree of five objects (about 10 KB and 60 µs each). A prototype's drawables are described once (`drawableTemplates`: each drawable with its matrix relative to the prototype root, in clone traversal order); the batches, picking (stand-in meshes for the few broadphase candidates, so a hit still carries its world matrix and material), bounds, selection boxes (a `BoxHelper` filled with the same box `setFromObject` gave), region selection, collision extraction and path-traced stills (stable stand-in meshes kept while Photo traces) all work from a record plus its templates. The edge mode is one setting for all handles. A real tree is created only where a part must be drawn or moved on its own — moving mechanism parts and transient transform poses (the batches' dynamic set), and every occurrence under `?referenceRenderer=1` — and follows its record while it exists.

- **Buckets are built once, then refilled.** Every drawable of every handle, visible or not, is classified once per model update into buckets keyed by geometry, base material and render order. Visibility (instruction steps, layers, floor focus, Play inclusion, edge modes), treatments (layer ghosting, floor ghosting, instruction dimming), transforms (explode) and section cuts only rewrite each bucket's instance matrices and counts in its existing draws. Before, each of these rebuilt every batch, and a dimmed or ghosted part fell back to one draw per drawable (about 48,000 draws for an instruction step of the 20,000-part fixture).
- **Treatments are instanced.** `LayerGhost` registers each transparent clone with its base material (`registerTreatment`), so a ghosted or dimmed occurrence stays in its bucket and moves to a second draw of that bucket with the clone material (depth write off, drawn after opaque parts, sorted per draw).
- **Transparent parts are instanced** like opaque ones. Instances of one bucket share one material, so the order in which they blend does not change the colour; draws are still sorted against each other. A bucket with a single occurrence is drawn as a plain copy.
- **Section cuts** also leave out instances wholly beyond the plane; the clipping plane still cuts the parts it crosses.
- **Adaptive quality while moving.** Above `motionReductionTriangles` scene triangles (desktop 4 M, phone 500 k; see `render-budget.ts`), frames drawn while the view moves (orbit, zoom, pinch, transform drags, Play camera) leave out edge and conditional lines by hiding their batch draws (no refill), and phones draw at ≤ 1.25× pixel density (`MOTION_PIXEL_RATIO_CAP`). The view redraws once at full quality `MOTION_IDLE_MS` (180 ms) after the last motion, or when the drag ends. Captures never use the reduced state. `render.budget()` reports `motion` and the last frame's draw statistics.
- **Shadow maps are cached.** Shadows (a look's soft shadows or the photo quality profile) re-render only when the scene changes. Camera-only frames, including Play frames where only the camera or the figure (which casts no shadow) moved, reuse the map; moving mechanism parts re-render it. `lastFrame.shadowPasses` counts shadow renders.
- **Indexed part geometry.** After compilation, each new prototype's triangle meshes are indexed once (`src/render/geometry-index.ts`): corners with bit-identical attributes merge, so the vertex shader runs once per shared corner. Groups, picking (`face.a/b/c`), collision extraction and baked batches read indexed geometry already.
- **Picking** tests triangle meshes only, after a per-handle box broadphase ordered by distance with an early exit, and no longer recomputes every world matrix per pick.
- **Hidden-geometry culling.** Studs received by an opaque part above, undersides resting on closed-shell parts (plain bricks, plates, tiles), parts enclosed on all six faces, and undersides whose downward opening faces away from the eye are not drawn: the LDraw counterpart of Minebench's hidden-face culling. A covered 2 × 4 brick in a wall draws 18 of its 700 triangles. Variants share the part's vertex buffers (only the index differs); the batches pick per refill what is exact for the view (neighbour-dependent culling only among the parts drawn solid and in place, never with a section cut). When the parts shown change (an instruction step, a floor focus, a hidden layer, Play), the next draw classifies once more so that view is culled against what it shows; explode and sections need no rebuild, and see-through ghosting that would exceed the budget is left out ([INSTRUCTIONS.md](INSTRUCTIONS.md#performance-step-views-are-culled-against-what-they-show)). Budgets count plain-view triangles with every opening in sight. Picking, collision, connectors and Photo read full geometry. `render.budget()` reports `hiddenGeometry` and `batches.culled/omitted/plainView`; `?hiddenCull=0` turns it off. Design and measurements: [PERFORMANCE-MINEBENCH.md](PERFORMANCE-MINEBENCH.md).
- **Adaptive culling cells.** Buckets drawing at least 16,384 triangles (or line segments) in all and holding at least 64 instances are also split by a spatial grid sized from the model's extent (about six cells across its largest dimension, none under 160 LDU). Heaviest buckets are split first, up to 1,024 cell groups in all (each is an extra draw). Whether a frame draws the cells or one draw per bucket is decided per frame from the view: `culledShare(camera)` is the share of those buckets' primitives lying in grid cells wholly outside the camera's frustum (one box test per occupied cell). Cells turn on at 50 % and off below 30 %: a first-person or Play view inside a large build culls the cells behind and beside the camera; a whole-model view keeps one draw per bucket, where cells only multiply draw calls. Switching refills the instance arrays; nothing is rebuilt. Captures, and ghosted or dimmed views (which would need a second draw per cell), always draw whole buckets. On the 20,000-part village this culls 18–21 % of the triangles while walking into it in Play and 13 % with an editor camera in its middle; whole-model views are unchanged (see [verification](VERIFICATION.md#lighter-handles-prepared-frames-and-adaptive-culling-30-september-2026)). `render.budget().batches` reports `cells`, `cellSize` and `cellsInUse`. `?batchCells=<LDU>` still forces cells of a fixed size (a diagnostic) and `?batchCells=0` turns them off.
- **Prepared frames.** A model update classifies the batches (`build()`), fills them (`prepare()`, which also makes the cell decision) and warms the shader programs they need, each in tasks of its own, while frames are held (the canvas keeps its last image, at most 10 s). Warming creates the programs for one drawable per distinct material and object kind (not every draw: `compile()` of a whole large scene cost 0.7 s of parameter building), then, without `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`, waits behind a fence (polled, not blocking) for the GPU to work through what is queued, and takes each new program's first-use status and uniform queries one per task. With the extension it waits for each program's completion status instead. A rebuild is deferred until then: frames keep drawing the previous fill instead of classifying 20,000 handles themselves. Draw objects and their instance buffers are reused across rebuilds when a bucket with the same geometry needs them.

## Exploded views

Two view-only exploded views share _Camera views_ → _Cut_; one is on at a time. **Explode floors** lifts floors (top-level submodels, else layers) straight up. **Anatomy** takes the model apart by its own submodels (else layers, else touching clusters): the base stays, every other group slides out along ±X, ±Z or up — whichever clears the parts in its way soonest — mirrored pairs move as mirror images, and groups leave one after another. Both only move occurrence handle translations and refill the batches; nothing is rebuilt and the document is unchanged. Design, planning rules and measurements: [ANATOMY.md](ANATOMY.md).

## Textured parts

Official parts that use the LDraw `!TEXMAP` extension ([spec](https://www.ldraw.org/texmap-spec.html)) are drawn with their printed images: 179 top-level parts of the complete library (sticker sheets, printed tiles and bricks, minifig and Duplo heads and torsos, sails, Bionicle discs, a printed baseplate) mapping 138 images. Before, the loader ignored the extension, so these parts drew their fallback geometry (or, like `87079pxf`, a hole where the print goes).

**What is supported.** `0 !TEXMAP START | NEXT | FALLBACK | END` with `PLANAR`, `CYLINDRICAL` and `SPHERICAL` projections, `0 !:` texture-only geometry lines (including `0 !: 1` subfile references, which inherit the texture), nested textures (a stack; `END` pops), `NEXT` for exactly one geometry line, textures ending at `STEP` and at the end of their file, quoted file names, and the `textures/` search prefix. The image's alpha lets the part colour show through, and outside the texture's extent (coordinates beyond 0…1) the face is the plain part colour. `GLOSSMAP` is parsed but not drawn (no official file uses one), and embedded `!DATA` images are not rendered.

**How it works.** `src/render/texmap.ts` parses the command and holds the projection maths; the vendored loader (`vendor/LDrawLoader.js`, patches marked "Brick Editor patch") tracks the texture state per file, computes image-space texture coordinates for every textured face in the coordinates of the file that started the texture (a subfile referenced inside the section is mapped after it is transformed into that file), and emits textured geometry as separate objects tagged `userData.texmap = { t, m }`: `t` the image (with a `uv` attribute) and `m` when it is drawn (`T` only with textures: `0 !:` lines; `F` only without: the `FALLBACK` section; `B` both: plain lines between `START` and `FALLBACK`). This runs in the compile workers; part records (`part-record.ts`) carry the tags and UVs unchanged, and `PART_COMPILER_VERSION` is `bpc4`. On the main thread `src/render/texmap-textures.ts` resolves each prototype once: when every image it names is available it drops the `F` objects and draws the textured ones with a `TexmapMaterial` (the part colour's own material plus the image, blended in the fragment shader), else it drops the `T` objects and draws the fallback. Textured materials are shared per (colour material, image) and images per name, so every occurrence, colour and part using an image shares one texture, and instancing, batching, ghosting and dimming treat textured meshes like any other.

- PLANAR: point 1 is the image's top-left corner, 2 its top-right, 3 its bottom-left. CYLINDRICAL: u is the angle about the axis 1→2 from the radial 1→3 over the extent `a` (right-handed, so the image reads left to right from outside), v the height from the base (image top at the top). SPHERICAL: u is the angle within the plane of points 1, 2 and 3 from 1→2 over `a`; v is the elevation above that plane over `b`, with the image top towards (1→2) × (1→3). The spec measures v as the angle within the perpendicular plane, which equals the elevation for points on that plane but jumps to ±180° just behind the centre; the elevation is used everywhere. Polygons that straddle the ±180° seam are kept on one side.
- Checked against real parts: the tile `87079pxf` reads "DEFENDER" the right way round, minifig head `3626cpx0` shows its face on the front, and globe `61287p01` shows the Americas unmirrored.

**Images and budgets.** Images come from the complete library's texture pack (`public/libraries/textures-ldraw-full-2026-09-28`, see [status](STATUS.md#textured-parts-2026-09-30)), fetched only when a textured part is drawn, verified against the pinned manifest and kept in Cache Storage, so a textured model that rendered once renders offline. They are decoded with `createImageBitmap` (straight alpha, no colour conversion), downscaled so the longer side is at most 2,048 px on desktop and 1,024 px on phones (`textureSize` in `render-budget.ts`), uploaded as sRGB with mipmaps, clamped at the edges and 8× (phones 4×) anisotropic. Decoded memory (RGBA with mipmaps) counts against `textureBytes`, 256 MiB on desktop and 64 MiB on phones; an image that would exceed it is not decoded, and its part draws the fallback. `render.budget().textures` reports images, decoded bytes, the budget, the size limit and how many parts drew textured or fallback.

**Fallback and diagnostics.** A missing image (offline and never cached, a failed hash check, over budget, not in the library) makes the part draw its fallback geometry. `ready()` then returns an `UNSUPPORTED_RENDER_FEATURE` warning naming the parts and the reason, strict readiness and strict captures refuse, and Model health lists it under Rendering features. Parts drawn textured report nothing. Texture mapping inside a custom (project) part compiles the same way and draws when its image names an official image; texture mapping in an ordinary model (over part references) is kept but not drawn, and the import reports it.

**Cost.** The five images of `fixtures/ldraw/textured-parts.ldr` decode to 15.7 MB on desktop and 7.6 MB on phones (the 1,600² globe image becomes 1,024²). A textured part draws one more instanced draw per image; its record also stores the fallback geometry (both forms are compiled once, the unused one is dropped when the prototype is made) and 8 bytes of texture coordinates per textured vertex. Untextured parts compile to exactly the objects they did before. The largest image is 4,746 × 2,301 (`6280006a.png`, 10.9 MP); all 138 decoded at desktop size would need about 384 MiB, over the budget, which only a model using nearly every textured part would reach.

**Not done.** The path-traced Photo look traces textured faces in their part colour (the tracer's materials carry no images); gloss maps; embedded `!DATA` images; texture mapping over part references in ordinary models; a model that references a textured part built as a separate model (a `Part`, not a subpart, inside a texture section) keeps that part's own textures rather than projecting the outer one.

## Photo: path-traced stills

User feedback from a real phone: "Realistic look is fine but Photo mode isn't very different." Photo was Realistic plus 32 jittered raster samples, so it only gained anti-aliasing and softer shadows. It is now a path tracer ([three-gpu-pathtracer](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer) 0.0.23 with [three-mesh-bvh](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh) 0.7.8, both MIT, bundled through npm; 0.0.23 is the last release that supports three r174). Source: `src/render/photo-tracer.ts` (the tracer, loaded on first use as its own chunk), `src/render/photo-denoise.ts` (the denoiser), `src/render/photo-sampling.ts` (random numbers and the fixes to the library's sampler), `src/render/photo-policy.ts` (fallback choice, scheduling, trace size, noise estimate and stop rule, lens), the adapter's `drawPhotoFrame`/`startPhoto`/`capturePathTraced`. See [Photo speed](#photo-speed-time-to-a-clean-still) for how a still gets clean quickly.

What makes it a photograph rather than a raster frame:

- **Light transport.** Up to 4 diffuse/glossy bounces (3 on phones) and 6 transmission bounces (4 on phones): colour bleeds from bricks onto the baseplate and neighbouring parts, crevices and stud bases darken physically instead of through screen-space AO, and shadows are soft because the lights have area.
- **Studio lighting.** The procedural HDR studio shared with Realistic (`src/render/studio-lighting.ts`, 512×256 float, generated on the CPU once per page, no asset): a key softbox above the left of the framing camera, a dim fill to the right, two strip lights behind for rim highlights and an overhead soft light, over dark walls. It is fixed in the world (it used to turn with the camera's azimuth; see [one studio](#one-studio-for-realistic-and-photo)). It is importance-sampled (multiple importance sampling), so softbox highlights and penumbrae converge quickly.
- **Staging.** A seamless studio sweep (`backdrop: "studio"`): the floor under the model curves up into a wall all the way round (an infinity cove turned into a bowl), single-sided and facing inwards, so there is no horizon from any orbit angle. It is a light, slightly glossy grey tinted towards the background colour, so it carries the model's soft shadow and a faint reflection. `backdrop: "floor"` uses a large floor under the plain background colour instead. Transparent-background captures make the floor and cove _matte_: they still block and bounce light but are cut out, so the cut-out has studio lighting without a ground. A scene environment composes with Photo instead of fighting it: objects added to the scene with `userData.photoStage = true` (a ground, props) are traced in place of the generated sweep (`backdrop` resolves to `scene`), with their material's colour, roughness and metalness, or a `userData.photoMaterial` (`MeshPhysicalMaterial`) they supply; textures are not traced. The sweep is used only when no environment provides a stage (Blank/Studio).
- **Materials.** ABS is a dielectric (IOR 1.53, roughness 0.32) with a thin clear coat (0.45, roughness 0.08) for the crisp moulded highlight; transparent parts refract (transmission 1, IOR 1.52; the surface tint is the square root of the colour, because light crosses two surfaces, so dark transparent colours such as trans dark blue keep their tint instead of turning black); chrome, metal, matte metallic, pearl and rubber get metal/roughness values per finish; glow-in-the-dark parts emit. Ghosted or dimmed parts stay see-through with their treatment opacity. Glitter and speckle have no flakes in the path tracer (their raster shader hooks do not apply); glitter is traced as slightly rough transparent plastic.
- **Lens.** A thin lens with a six-blade aperture focused on the last tapped part (a pick sets the focus point), else on the centre of the visible model's bounds, else the orbit target. The aperture radius is `depthOfField × 0.03 ×` the focus distance, so a small model close up and a large one from afar blur alike relative to their size (default 0.25: the model stays sharp, the foreground and the sweep soften).
- **Film.** PBR Neutral tone mapping at 0.8× exposure, like Realistic, so LEGO colours stay saturated (AgX, available as `toneMapping: "agx"`, desaturated red roofs), a film grade (a gentle S-curve and +15 % saturation), a vignette of 0.2. The traced image is denoised before tone mapping (see [Photo speed](#photo-speed-time-to-a-clean-still)).

Interaction. Any change (orbit, zoom, edit, resize) draws a realistic raster frame with the same tone mapping and grade at once. Path tracing starts once the view has been still for `PHOTO_IDLE_MS` (220 ms) and no pointer is held, and replaces the raster frame after 2 samples. The still is traced at up to 1.6 MP on desktops and 0.48 MP on phones (`tracePixels`) and upscaled. Each sample is traced in 2×2 tiles; the work per animation frame adapts to the measured frame interval (halved above 70 ms, doubled below 22 ms, one more tile below 36 ms, up to 4 whole samples per frame on fast GPUs), and a one-pixel readback after each frame keeps tiles from queueing ahead of the GPU, so input stays responsive. Each whole sample is denoised and presented once. The toast shows "Preparing photo…" (BVH and shader) and then "Refining photo… n%" (against the samples the still is expected to need, from its measured noise), and says "Photo refined: N path-traced samples." when the still is clean or at `pathSamples`. Drawing then stops. A change that does not affect the still (a selection change) keeps its samples; selection boxes, gizmos and measurements are drawn over the traced image. Play never path-traces.

The BVH is rebuilt only when the traced content changes (a signature of visible meshes, their materials and world matrices), in a worker (every model scene, so a build never blocks input; the main thread builds it if the worker fails); camera moves only reset the samples. The tracing shader compiles while the BVH builds. Leaving the Photo look frees the BVH, vertex textures and accumulation targets but keeps the compiled shader, so switching back or capturing again does not recompile. Context loss disposes the tracer.

Captures (`render.image({ look: "photo" })`, CLI `--look photo`) trace exactly `pathSamples` samples at the full capture size (no stop rule, no downscale) with the same stage, lens and denoiser (captures above 4.2 MP, 2.1 MP on phones, skip the denoiser's targets). Noise is seeded (`PhotoSampler` restarts its sequence on reset), so the same view and sample count trace the same image within a page. Tests compare with a tolerance, not bytes.

Side-by-side captures of one camera (Standard, Realistic, Photo at 64 samples, 640×480): [finishes fixture](screenshots/photo/finishes-standard-realistic-photo.png), [House with garden](screenshots/photo/house-standard-realistic-photo.png), [Small castle](screenshots/photo/castle-standard-realistic-photo.png). Transparent colours over a dark part stay dark in Photo, as they do physically (the castle's trans-dark-blue moat reads navy, where Realistic's alpha blending lightens it).

### Photo speed: time to a clean still

User feedback from a real phone: "Photo look rendering currently takes about 30 seconds to render just the buggy" (the Off-road jeep, about 80 parts). Profiling on the VM found three bugs and three missing pieces:

1. **The phone never converged.** three-gpu-pathtracer 0.0.23 sizes its per-sample random-tuple texture `bounces + transmissiveBounces + 5` texels wide, but its shader reads tuples up to index 16. With the phone's 4 + 4 bounces (13 wide) tuples 13–16 were read outside the texture: zero plus the pixel's fixed offset in every sample. Tuple 15 picks the BSDF lobe (diffuse, specular, clear coat, transmission), so every pixel always took the same lobe: a fixed speckle pattern that no number of samples removed (at the 64-sample cap the phone still had a mean error of 4.3 levels against its own 1024-sample image, the desktop 1.0). `tupleTextureSize` now makes the texture wide enough for every tuple the shader reads (checked against the shader in a unit test).
2. **The sampler was biased for its first 64 samples.** After a reset the library's stratified sampler walks its 8⁴ strata in order, so the third and fourth dimension of every tuple stay in the first eighth of [0, 1) for 64 and 512 samples. `PhotoSampler` replaces it: per tuple two R2 low-discrepancy sequences with seeded offsets, their order permuted in blocks of 16 so bounces stay uncorrelated; seeded, so captures repeat. Raw (undenoised) error against a 1024-sample reference, jeep: 17.1 / 8.2 levels at 8 / 64 samples with the library's sampler, 10.9 / 3.4 with R2.
3. **Each pixel rotated all sample dimensions by one blue-noise value**, so neighbouring pixels' errors were correlated along the diagonal of the sample space (tinted patches rather than grain, which a spatial filter cannot remove). `decorrelatePixels` patches the shader to rotate each of the four dimensions by its own shifted copy of the blue noise.
4. **Four compiles of the 115 KB tracing shader instead of one.** The library recompiles on every define change and compiled for the canvas (its tone mapping and colour space) while it draws into a linear float target, so a first still linked four variants. `compile()` now settles the defines first and compiles once, for the float target, and starts while the BVH builds (in a worker for every model scene; before, below 60,000 triangles, e.g. the jeep, it blocked the main thread). SwiftShader compiles lazily and does not show the saving; drivers that link eagerly (phones) pay for every variant.
5. **A real denoiser** (`photo-denoise.ts`): a spatial SVGF-style filter. Guide buffers (normal, view depth, albedo, depth gradient, packed into one float texture) are rasterised from the traced geometry itself (so the studio sweep and scene grounds are in them; glass and see-through parts are left out, so what is behind them guides the filter). The traced image is demodulated by albedo (colour edges between coplanar bricks stay sharp), then five 3×3 à-trous passes (steps 1–16 px) filter it with normal, depth and luminance edge-stopping weights, then it is remodulated. The luminance weight scales with the per-pixel noise, measured from the difference between the accumulation and a snapshot at half the sample count (pure noise, no signal), so the filter backs off by itself as the still converges; the variance is filtered alongside the image (SVGF). Out-of-focus pixels (thin-lens blur circle over about a pixel) are filtered across the sharp guides' edges and without demodulation, so depth of field does not leave sharp outlines. Cost: 6 full-screen passes per whole sample at the traced size.
6. **A stop rule instead of a fixed sample count.** Every 4 samples the presented (denoised, tone-mapped) image is probed on a ~24,000-pixel grid and compared with the probe at half the samples: for nested averages the difference has variance σ²(1/m − 1/n), which gives the current noise without a reference (`estimateNoise`, robust median-absolute-deviation so the few edge pixels the filter sharpens do not count). The still stops once that noise is below 1.5 display levels on desktops and 2 on phones, after at least 8 samples; `pathSamples` (256 / 64) stays the upper bound. Progress in the toast is measured against the samples the noise predicts.

Also: interactive stills are traced at most 1.6 MP (desktop) or 0.48 MP (phones) and upscaled (a 1080×1800 phone drew about 1.1 MP before); bounces 4 on desktops and 3 on phones (5 and 4 before; the mean difference to 5 bounces at 34 samples is 1.3 levels for 3 and for 4 bounces, within the noise); up to 4 whole samples per frame on fast GPUs (before, at most one); each whole sample is denoised and presented once rather than after every tile.

## How it is built

- **Environment**: the [shared studio](#one-studio-for-realistic-and-photo), a half-float equirectangular image prefiltered with `PMREMGenerator.fromEquirectangular` at first use and kept (one map per backdrop ground colour, at most two). Nothing is fetched and the site stays self-contained under its CSP. The maps are regenerated after a context loss, because render-target contents are lost. With the studio the hemisphere light drops to 0.12, the fill light is off and the key light is the studio's key softbox (`LOOK_LIGHTING`, `rasterStudioLight()`). `environment: "room"` keeps three's `RoomEnvironment` with the old 0.25/2.0/0.2 hemisphere/key/fill.
- **Materials**: LDrawLoader's `MeshStandardMaterial`s are tuned in place and restored exactly from `userData.lookBase`. In-place tuning keeps batching keys, prototype sharing and the layer-ghost/instruction-dimming clones working. Finishes are classified from the loader's finish values and the LDConfig name (`FINISH_PARAMETERS`):

  | Finish                       | Roughness | Metalness | Env. intensity |
  | ---------------------------- | --------- | --------- | -------------- |
  | Plastic (ABS)                | 0.28      | 0         | 1              |
  | Transparent                  | 0.04      | 0         | 1.6            |
  | Chrome                       | 0.06      | 1         | 1.2            |
  | Pearlescent                  | 0.32      | 0.55      | 1.1            |
  | Metal                        | 0.28      | 0.9       | 1.1            |
  | Matte metallic               | 0.48      | 0.7       | 1              |
  | Rubber                       | 0.82      | 0         | 0.55           |
  | Glitter (transparent) flakes | 0.06      | 0         | 1.6            |
  | Speckle                      | 0.3       | 0         | 1              |

  LDConfig `MATERIAL GLITTER|SPECKLE` lines, which LDrawLoader ignores, become procedural world-space flakes in an `onBeforeCompile` hook with its own program cache key. Glitter flakes are mirror-like. Speckle density is halved and flakes are enlarged 1.2×, because cube cells at the stated density read as noise.

- **Pipeline** (`LookPipeline`): the scene is drawn into a half-float HDR target (4× MSAA on desktop, 2× on phones) that has a depth texture. Three's `GTAOPass` then runs on the **resolved beauty depth**, with normals reconstructed from depth, at half resolution and clipped to the model's bounds. AO therefore costs no second geometry pass. The r174 constructor crashes when given a depth texture, so the adapter sets it afterwards. A composite pass tone-maps (PBR Neutral), composites over the background, multiplies AO, adds dark AO/shadow coverage over transparent backgrounds, and applies the vignette. With accumulation, it blends into a premultiplied running average in ping-pong half-float targets, and a present pass encodes to the canvas or the sRGB capture target.
- **Shadows**: the key light's orthographic shadow frustum is fitted to the model's bounding sphere, instead of the fixed ±1500 LDU. The map is at least 2048² (1024² on phones), so shadows are crisp on small models. A `ShadowMaterial` plane at the model's lowest point receives shadows and writes depth for contact AO. The grid is dimmed to 45%.
- **Tone mapping**: ACES bleached sunlit yellow and red tops to cream, so the realistic looks use Khronos PBR Neutral, with exposure × 0.85 of the quality profile's.

Also found: **direct (Standard) captures are not tone-mapped.** Three applies tone mapping only when drawing to the canvas, so `render.image` output from the Standard look is linear→sRGB without ACES/exposure, whatever the manifest's `toneMapping` says. The manifest now reports `output.toneMapped`. Look-pipeline captures are tone-mapped. The Standard capture path was left unchanged to keep existing images byte-identical.
